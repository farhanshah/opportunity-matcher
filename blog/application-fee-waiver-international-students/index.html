Application Fee Waivers for International Students: How to Check

Three verified university policies, one practical decision process, and no promises of automatic eligibility.

OppMatcher editorial · Official-source comparisonReviewed 16 November 2026
The short answer An international student may qualify for a university application fee waiver, but not every institution offers one and the rules are rarely universal. Before you submit, separate three questions: does the course charge a fee, does your exact applicant category qualify for a waiver, and is approval automatic or requested in advance? These are university admission fees, not scholarship awards.

First, separate a university application fee from scholarship funding

A student can receive a scholarship and still need to pay a university application fee to be considered for admission. Another university may waive the fee for a particular group of applicants without promising them any scholarship. A third may offer a scholarship application at no cost but require payment for the degree application. These are different decisions made by different offices.

When someone asks “Are there scholarships with no application fee?”, the useful answer is to identify which application has a charge. Search the official university and funder websites separately. Do not confuse a tuition waiver, funding award, or deposit exemption with permission to skip an application fee.

Notice the practical difference: Cambridge publishes an automatic route for defined applicants, while Stanford school-based programmes can require advance review, and Princeton provides its own request categories. Copying one university's method into another application will not work.

Cambridge example: nationality alone may not be enough

Cambridge's current postgraduate fee-waiver page says eligible international applicants must meet its specified nationality and residence conditions, including a rule about having lived in an eligible country since birth. Some applicants who have moved between eligible countries or experienced displacement are directed to a separate request route. It also lists distinct circumstances for UK applicants and other special programmes.

This means an applicant should not assume that holding a passport from a lower-income country automatically qualifies them. Cambridge updates its country list, so use the official fee-waiver eligibility page for the intended intake. Cambridge also warns that a fee waiver cannot be granted after the application fee has already been paid, and that some request routes require time for review before a course or funding deadline.

Princeton example: request before paying

Princeton's Graduate School identifies particular waiver bases including financial hardship and qualifying programme participation. Its application tips tell candidates to request a waiver at application time and wait for the decision before submitting and paying. The guidance says an application fee is not refundable. A request by itself is not an approved waiver.

If a portal displays a fee after you send a request, do not assume that the payment screen is a software mistake. Read the university's confirmation and contact the admissions help channel through the official site when the result is unclear.

Stanford example: check the school and programme

Stanford's Graduate School of Education, Engineering, Humanities and Sciences, and Medicine do not all apply identical school-based fee-waiver rules. Some review international candidates case by case for certain degree types, while another type of master's application may be excluded. Deadlines for review can be earlier than the course deadline.

Use the official school-based waiver page and check the exact school or department before writing a financial-hardship statement. A strong personal statement cannot replace the published eligibility rule.

How to request an application fee waiver without delaying submission

  1. Open the official course application instructions. Record the degree, entry year and fee amount, if any. Do not rely on a blog's older fee list.
  2. Identify the waiver mechanism. It may be automatic, available via the application portal, linked to a separate form, limited to programme participants, or unavailable.
  3. Read the eligibility definition literally. Country of citizenship, residence, previous participation, financial need and degree type are not interchangeable.
  4. Collect only requested evidence. This might be an approved programme-participation certificate or financial-hardship documentation. Never email sensitive documents to an unverified address.
  5. Plan for review time. Check whether the request must be submitted days or weeks before the degree or scholarship deadline.
  6. Wait for the official decision if instructed. Save the approval message, then confirm the fee is removed from the portal before submission.
  7. Keep an alternative plan. If the waiver is refused, consider the official fee and timing before deciding whether to apply.

What to ask if the university's fee rule is unclear

A short factual enquiry is enough: identify your intended degree and entry year, state the waiver category you think may apply, and ask which official route and deadline to use. Do not claim hardship you cannot document or ask an unrelated scholarship office to bypass admissions rules.

For example: “I am preparing an application for [programme and intake]. I have read your published fee-waiver policy and would like to confirm whether applicants in [specified category] should use the application-portal request or a separate form. What is the latest date to submit the request before the admissions deadline?”

Frequently asked questions

Does a fully funded scholarship automatically cover the university application fee?

No. The scholarship's funding package, admission fee and fee-waiver policy must be checked separately. An award may only take effect after admission.

Can international students request a fee waiver?

Sometimes. Cambridge has specific country and residence rules; Stanford schools differ by degree and applicant category. The correct answer depends on the official programme policy, not a blanket statement about nationality.

Can I request a waiver after paying the fee?

Do not assume so. Cambridge says a waiver cannot be given after payment, and Princeton describes its fee as nonrefundable. Request and wait for a decision in the order specified by the institution.

Should I apply to programmes just because they are free to apply to?

No. Check academic fit, tuition and living costs, scholarship eligibility, required documents and the real application deadline. Saving a fee is useful only if the programme is a credible match.

Turn the fee check into a useful shortlist

For each programme, keep a line with the official application URL, fee, waiver category, evidence needed, request deadline, response status and programme deadline. Add a separate row for scholarship funding if its conditions differ.
