Chevening leadership essay: evidence and preparation

Understand the criteria before writing your own answer

What Chevening assesses in leadership

Chevening asks applicants to demonstrate leadership and influencing skills through their own experience. A job title or a list of responsibilities is not enough: the selection guidance looks for examples where your actions helped change an outcome, solve a problem or achieve a result. You might draw evidence from professional work, volunteering or a community project, provided your account is accurate.

Read the official Chevening application criteria before deciding which experience to discuss. Follow the actual application form for the current question, length limit and required format.

Find evidence from something you really did

Use the prompts below privately to recall events. They are a planning worksheet, not a scholarship answer or text to submit.

  1. The problem: What was happening, who was affected and why did it matter? Keep the background short.
  2. Your responsibility: What could you personally influence? Distinguish your work from your colleagues' or organisation's contribution.
  3. Your action: Which decisions did you make? How did you involve others, handle disagreement or persuade people to change course?
  4. The result: What changed, and what evidence supports that conclusion? A verified outcome is stronger than an invented percentage or an unsupported success claim.
  5. Your learning: What would you do differently, and how has the experience influenced your next steps?

Choose one or two substantial examples instead of listing every project you have participated in. If you cannot explain your individual action or its outcome, look for a different event. Chevening's own application advice discusses the importance of a clear personal contribution.

What counts as influence?

Influence can mean convincing a group to try a better process, negotiating support for a community activity, removing a barrier or helping colleagues reach a workable agreement. It need not mean supervising a large team. Equally, completing a task assigned by a manager does not automatically demonstrate influence: the detail that matters is what you initiated, decided or changed.

For example, when reviewing your experiences, distinguish “I attended planning meetings” from a verifiable record of a particular decision you proposed and what happened after the team tried it. This is a distinction for self-assessment, not suggested application wording.

Common mistakes to check before you write

  • Repeating a CV instead of addressing the leadership criterion.
  • Using “we” for an entire achievement without identifying your own role.
  • Choosing a prestigious organisation instead of the experience with the clearest evidence.
  • Making up numbers, changing facts or implying decisions you did not make.
  • Ignoring the current official prompt, question scope and word limit.
  • Using a sample or machine-written answer, which Chevening does not permit.

Chevening's rule about AI-written applications

Chevening states that applicants must not use AI software to generate their application answers. Do not paste a generated essay, rewrite machine-generated paragraphs to disguise their origin or submit another person's sample. This page provides reading guidance and reflection questions only. Your application response must be your original work in your own words. Check the current official policy for details.

The official application timeline shows the 2027/28 scholarship round closed in October 2026; this guide is not a claim that applications are currently open.
