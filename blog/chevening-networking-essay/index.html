Chevening networking essay: relationship evidence

Show how real professional relationships produced results

What Chevening means by relationship-building

Chevening asks applicants to show that they can build and maintain professional relationships and use those relationships constructively. Networking is more than attending a conference or collecting contacts. The official criteria focus on how you developed trust, collaborated with others and achieved positive outcomes through your connections.

Use the Chevening application criteria to check the current standard, then read your actual application form for the precise question and word limit. Your answer must come from your own experience.

Build an evidence map from real experiences

The following questions help you identify a suitable event. They are for personal preparation, not a paragraph template or finished essay.

  1. How it began: Where did you meet the person or group, and what shared purpose brought you together?
  2. Your contribution: Did you share knowledge, connect people, follow up on commitments or help resolve a problem? Identify exactly what you did.
  3. Reciprocity: How did the other person or group benefit? What did you gain or learn? Avoid portraying networking as simply asking others for favours.
  4. Continuity: How did you maintain the relationship after the initial event? Describe real communication or subsequent collaboration, if there was any.
  5. Outcome: What happened because the relationship existed? Prefer observable and truthful results, even if they are modest.

If the only evidence is “I know many professionals,” look for a more substantial experience. Chevening's published application advice encourages applicants to explain how they formed relationships, sustained them and achieved something through them.

Professional relationships beyond formal employment

You may have built useful relationships through employment, a volunteer organisation, an alumni initiative, mentoring or an organised community project. A real collaboration matters more than an impressive event title. For each candidate example, check whether you can explain what changed after the first interaction and whether your contribution is distinct from the group's general activities.

Do not add fictional meetings, endorsements or partnerships to make an experience seem stronger. If you were primarily a participant, say so when deciding whether that example genuinely fits the criterion.

Connect your experience to future collaboration

Chevening also wants candidates who can contribute to a professional community. Reflect on an issue you intend to work on after your studies, the kinds of expertise you would need and what you could contribute in return. Keep the plan realistic and consistent with your stated study and career goals. A promise to “network with global leaders” without a purpose is less useful than knowing how collaboration would help address a specific problem.

Chevening's application guidance also asks applicants to explain their chosen UK courses and future plans. Make sure those elements are consistent, rather than treating four application criteria as unrelated stories.

Common mistakes

  • Describing the number of contacts instead of the value of a working relationship.
  • Focusing only on what another person did for you.
  • Reusing a leadership example without explaining its relationship-building aspect.
  • Claiming a relationship continued when there was no follow-up.
  • Making unrealistic promises about future connections or outcomes.
  • Copying sample wording or using AI-generated application answers.

Originality and application timing

Chevening prohibits AI-generated application responses. This page is intended to help you understand the criterion and recall your own genuine experiences. It does not provide text to submit. Consult the official originality rules before preparing your application.

According to the official timeline, applications for the 2027/28 scholarship round closed in October 2026. A guide about a programme does not mean its application portal is open.
