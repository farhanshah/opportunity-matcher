Chevening Work Experience: How to Check and Document It

Check current work-experience rules and build a truthful record of employment and voluntary activity.

OppMatcher editorial · Evidence-first applicationsPublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Check current work-experience rules and build a truthful record of employment and voluntary activity. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Use the present intake guidance

Scholarship eligibility rules can change. Rather than relying on an older online calculator or a past recipient's story, read the current Chevening eligibility and application instructions for your country. Check which kinds of work the scheme recognises.

Build a chronological work history

Record organisation, role, dates, hours and responsibilities for each period. Identify any overlaps so you do not count the same working hour twice. If the provider asks for minimum hours or a defined period, use the actual current method and confirm any rounding rules.

Separate evidence from job titles

A part-time support role can involve genuine initiative, while a senior-sounding title can conceal limited involvement. Describe the work you actually performed, the people you worked with and results you can support. Avoid using invented figures or inflating volunteer hours.

Align stories across forms

Use the same dates and job details in your CV and scholarship application. Prepare contact information for reference checks when requested. For career essays, explain how professional experiences connect to your intended study and realistic next steps.

Practical example

Example: a candidate combined part-time paid work with student-society volunteering. They should document each role's hours and dates and check the current scheme's eligibility rules before calculating what counts.

What to check

  1. Read the current experience policy
  2. Check overlapping employment periods
  3. Keep a consistent CV and timeline

Common question

Does every hour of volunteering count toward Chevening's experience requirement?

Not automatically. Chevening defines which types of experience count and when they must have been gained. Check its current work-experience guidance, record dates and hours accurately, and do not double-count overlapping activities.

The current rule: 2,800 hours after your undergraduate degree

Chevening's official work-experience page requires at least 2,800 hours of work experience gained after you finished your undergraduate degree. Chevening describes this as roughly two years of full-time work on a 35-hour week, but the hours can be built up over a different period. For the cycle that page describes, Chevening says your undergraduate degree must have been completed by October 2024. An application that does not meet the requirement is rejected, not marked down.

Older blog posts, and some older Chevening news articles, say that experience gained before or during your degree also counts. The current eligibility pages say the experience must come after completion. Use the live eligibility page for your own cycle, and treat any older article, including a third-party calculator, as out of date wherever it disagrees.

What counts, and how many roles you can list

Type of experienceListed by Chevening?What to record
Full-time employmentYesEmployer, job title, start and end dates, contracted weekly hours
Part-time employmentYesActual weekly hours, not a full-time equivalent
Voluntary workYesOrganisation, regular weekly hours and dates; a contact who can confirm it
Paid or unpaid internshipsYesDates and weekly hours from the offer or completion letter
Work completed before you graduatedNo, under the current ruleLeave it out of the hours total. It can still be mentioned in essays as background.

You can enter up to fifteen employment periods, and the requirement does not have to be met in one job. If you are currently employed you do not need to resign before applying, but Chevening says it is your responsibility to make sure your employer will release you if you are selected.

How the application form calculates your total

The application system multiplies the number of weeks in each role by the hours per week you enter, then adds the periods together. That makes two inputs critical. Chevening's own list of common application errors names four mistakes: not reaching the minimum hours, entering the wrong number of weeks, entering the same experience more than once, and giving unrealistic weekly hours.

Worked example: two jobs and a volunteer role

Suppose an applicant graduated in June 2023 and has three periods of post-graduation experience:

  1. Graduate assistant, July 2023 to June 2024: 48 weeks × 20 hours = 960 hours.
  2. Full-time analyst, July 2024 to August 2025: 56 weeks × 35 hours = 1,960 hours.
  3. Weekend volunteer tutor, January to June 2025, 4 hours a week for 24 weeks = 96 hours.

The total is 960 + 1,960 + 96 = 3,016 hours. But the volunteer role overlaps with the analyst job. That is not double-counting the same hours, because the tutoring happened at weekends outside contracted work, so it can be entered as its own period. What would be double-counting is listing the analyst job twice, or entering 35 hours a week for a period you were actually part-time. Even without the volunteer role, this applicant has 2,920 hours, so they are not relying on borderline entries.

If your total only just reaches 2,800, check each period against a contract, payslip or letter before you submit. A small error in the week count can push you below the threshold.

Be ready to explain every entry

Chevening has said that applicants who reach interview are asked about the employment they listed, so you need to be able to talk about each role in detail. Keep a private file with the evidence for every period: contracts, payslips, appointment letters and a referee contact. Beyond eligibility, Chevening assesses the strength of the examples in your essays, and says that broader or more varied experience often helps. Hours make you eligible, but your examples are what get scored.

Before you enter your hours

  1. Confirm your undergraduate completion date against the cut-off on the current eligibility page.
  2. List only post-graduation periods, with real start and end dates.
  3. Count weeks actually worked, not calendar months multiplied by four.
  4. Use real weekly hours for part-time and volunteer roles.
  5. Check that no period appears twice and that overlapping roles really ran at different times.
  6. Keep evidence for each entry ready for interview.

Read Chevening's full eligibility criteria as well. Citizenship, the two-year return commitment and previous UK government-funded study can each rule an application out, however many hours you have. Our Chevening application guide covers course choice and essays.
