Commonwealth Master's Scholarships: Routes and Requirements

Find the right nomination route and make a realistic UK postgraduate study plan.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official sources

Find the right nomination route and make a realistic UK postgraduate study plan. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Understand the nomination process

Commonwealth funding schemes can involve nominating agencies, universities or other approved channels. The correct route can depend on your nationality and scholarship type. Read the relevant official page and identify who actually receives your application.

Separate course entry from award rules

The scholarship may have conditions linked to study level and eligible development areas, while a UK university has separate admission requirements. Read both documents. Record any language test, academic grade or graduation evidence required by the chosen course.

Make the study plan concrete

Explain a problem you want to work on, the skills you lack and how a particular degree would help. Connect your past education or work to that plan with specific examples. Avoid broad promises that cannot be linked to realistic professional steps.

Prepare references and documents early

Transcripts, references and official translations often need more time than writing the online form. The nominating organisation may have its own procedures and an earlier internal deadline. Verify everything against the current call rather than archived guides.

Before submitting your application

Make a copy of the current official call, record the programme name and route, and confirm the time zone of any stated deadline. Check nationality, degree level, language requirements and the documents needed for your particular programme. If the relevant details are missing, contact the provider rather than treating the programme as open or as a guaranteed personal match.

Current round: 2027/28 Commonwealth Master's scholarships

The Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) published applications for master's study beginning in September or October 2027. The stated round opened on 8 September 2026 and closed on 20 October 2026 at 16:00 BST. It is therefore closed as of 3 November 2026. Do not use the 2027 deadline as if it were a future opening for the next academic cycle.

The award supports applicants from specified eligible low- and middle-income Commonwealth countries who could not otherwise afford to study in the UK, with full-time taught master's study linked to development priorities. The exact eligible countries and the programme's rules are published by CSC and must be checked afresh for every intake.

Two application routes, not one: CSC Central and a nominator

The key difference between this award and many direct-entry scholarships is that a candidate must submit to CSC Central and a participating nominator. CSC says it does not accept direct applications for these Master's awards without the appropriate nomination. Nominators include national nominating agencies and certain approved charities or non-governmental organisations.

CSC's official FAQ explicitly warns that nominating agencies may set different or earlier deadlines. Applicants must meet both deadlines and criteria.

Citizenship, residence and previous UK study

CSC's eligibility rules include citizenship or recognised refugee status in an eligible Commonwealth country (or British Protected Person status) and permanent residence in an eligible Commonwealth country. The award also expects applicants to begin their UK academic studies in the specified start period.

CSC says it would not normally fund a second master's degree without a strong justification. It separately excludes people who have already studied at master's level at a UK institution or received a Commonwealth Scholarship at master's level. These conditions matter even where the proposed course is academically suitable.

The scheme also involves an obligation to return home when the award ends. The published CSC conditions say a scholar cannot switch to a Graduate or other visa in order to remain in the UK after the scholarship. Confirm the current policy before accepting an award if your longer-term plans involve working in the UK.

A realistic example of the development-impact question

Suppose an applicant working in agricultural supply chains wants a master's in food systems. A strong development-impact plan would name a concrete gap they have observed, describe which techniques in the chosen course could help address it, and identify organisations or partners they could work with on return. An unsupported claim such as “I will transform agriculture in my country” provides little measurable evidence.

QuestionEvidence to prepare

What to do ahead of a future intake

  1. Check the CSC award page for the next published round.
  2. Confirm nationality, permanent residence, degree history and the required return condition.
  3. Identify the correct nominating agency and ask it for its independent rules and deadline.
  4. Review a suitable UK university course and admission deadlines, separately from scholarship deadlines.
  5. Prepare academic references, degree evidence and your own specific development-impact proposal.
  6. Only submit when the relevant cycle is officially open; use the free readiness checklist to identify missing evidence.
