Commonwealth Scholarships: How Nominating Agencies Work

Find the approved country channel before assuming you can apply directly to a Commonwealth scholarship.

OppMatcher editorial · Evidence-first applicationsPublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Find the approved country channel before assuming you can apply directly to a Commonwealth scholarship. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Identify the exact Commonwealth scheme

Different Commonwealth Scholarship Commission programmes can use nominating agencies, universities or other approved partners. Start from the current scheme page and verify which application route applies to your nationality and academic level.

Check the local deadline independently

A country's nominating body may request documents or interviews before the international provider's published deadline. Record both dates in your tracker and use the earlier as the practical deadline when it applies. Old ministry announcements are not evidence that a cycle is open.

Prepare nomination evidence

The agency may assess academic qualifications, study plans, development relevance and documents in its own format. Read the official country guidance, and avoid assuming that submitting to the UK funder replaces the national stage.

Understand what nomination means

Being nominated does not necessarily guarantee scholarship selection, admission to a UK university or immigration permission. Keep each decision separate and do not make non-refundable travel or study commitments before receiving binding information.

Practical example

Example: an applicant sees a Commonwealth award advertised globally, but the scheme uses their home country's nominating body. They should obtain the actual agency instructions before completing the central application.

What to check

  1. Find the scheme's authorised route
  2. Track local and central deadlines
  3. Keep nomination and university admission separate

Common question

Can I always apply for a Commonwealth scholarship without a nominating agency?

No single route applies to every Commonwealth scheme. Some use a country nominating agency, while other scholarships involve university-led selection. Confirm the current scheme and country instructions before applying.

Commonwealth Master's: you make two applications, not one

For Commonwealth Master's Scholarships, the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) says plainly that it does not accept direct applications. You apply to a nominator, the nominator runs its own selection, and it puts forward its shortlisted candidates to the CSC. You must also complete the CSC's own online application in CSC Central. A nominator's form or interview does not replace the CSC application, and the CSC application does not replace the nominator.

The CSC lists two kinds of nominator for this programme:

  • National nominating agencies, usually a ministry of education or higher-education body in your country. This is the main route.
  • Selected NGOs and charitable bodies. These routes can be narrow. For example, the CSC's NGO page says the Windle Trust International route is only for Master's candidates from Kenya and Uganda who meet that trust's criteria, and they must also apply to the trust separately.

Disabled applicants who do not hold a 2:1 but can show their potential to contribute to development may be considered for a contextualised nomination through the Commonwealth Disabled People's Forum. The CSC asks applicants to contact that nominator directly.

The deadline that matters is usually the agency's

The CSC's FAQ warns that the deadlines and criteria advertised by your nominating agency may differ from the CSC's, and that you must meet both. In practice, many agencies close weeks before the CSC deadline so they can shortlist and interview. If your agency closes in August and you only learn about the programme from the CSC's October deadline, you have missed this year's route.

Worked example: planning a 2028/29 application

Imagine an applicant who wants to start a UK Master's in September 2028. In early 2028 they read the CSC's 2027/28 page to understand the format, but they treat its dates only as a guide. When the 2028/29 cycle opens, they find their country's agency on the CSC's national nominating agency list. They then open the agency's own announcement, which might set a closing date earlier than the CSC's. They put the agency date first in their tracker and the CSC Central date second. They also start the CSC Central application as soon as it opens, because it has its own documents and its own deadline, and leaving it until after the agency stage can leave too little time.

This sequence is illustrative. Your agency may run a different order or ask for a separate form. Follow its published instructions exactly.

Eligibility that applies whichever nominator you use

The CSC's own criteria for the agency-nominated Master's programme include:

  • citizenship of an eligible Commonwealth country (or refugee status there, or British Protected Person status), and permanent residence in an eligible country;
  • availability to start in the UK by the September of the intake year;
  • a first degree of at least upper second-class (2:1), or a lower second plus a relevant postgraduate qualification, usually a Master's;
  • being unable to afford UK study without the scholarship;
  • not having already studied at Master's level in the UK or held a Commonwealth Master's Scholarship. A second Master's needs a justification.

Your agency may add its own requirements, such as an age limit, a priority subject list or work experience. Those are not CSC rules, but if you do not meet them you will not be nominated.

Nominating agency checklist

  1. Find your country on the CSC's current list of national nominating agencies, or check whether an NGO nominator covers you.
  2. Read the agency's own call for this year, not last year's notice.
  3. Record the agency deadline and the CSC Central deadline separately.
  4. Choose three UK courses in order of preference, as the CSC recommends.
  5. Prepare documents in the formats the CSC specifies.
  6. Do not pay anyone to "guarantee" a nomination. The CSC says it does not charge to apply through its online system.
