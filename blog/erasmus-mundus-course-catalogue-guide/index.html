Erasmus Mundus Course Catalogue: How to Shortlist Joint Master's Degrees

Select actual Erasmus Mundus courses before assessing scholarships, mobility and deadlines.

Select actual Erasmus Mundus courses before assessing scholarships, mobility and deadlines. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Start from the programme catalogue

Erasmus Mundus is a family of joint master's degrees run by university consortia, not a single subject or scholarship form. Identify courses matching your previous study and future goals. Open the consortium page for each one and record participating institutions and the degree award.

Check admissions before the scholarship

A programme may be academically open to candidates who are not selected for funding. Confirm academic prerequisites, language requirements, required references and the separate scholarship rules. Avoid assuming any consortium in the catalogue offers a scholarship for the intake you want.

Consider mobility and city costs

Some courses require study in two or more countries. Check the intended sequence of campuses, academic start dates and immigration steps. Housing costs and travel between universities can affect affordability even with funding, and the consortium should explain how the mobility arrangement works.

Create a useful shortlist

Compare three real course options in a table covering fit, host locations, scholarship selection and submission route. Eliminate a course if a mandatory entry condition is not met. Then request transcripts and references based on the most demanding application checklist rather than relying on one generic form.

Practical example

Example: a computer science graduate considering public policy and data programmes should compare the entry modules and expected quantitative background before choosing which Erasmus Mundus consortia are realistic.

  2. Separate admission and funding decisions
  3. Compare mobility locations
