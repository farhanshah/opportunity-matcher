Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters: How to Apply

Applications go to individual Erasmus Mundus course consortia, and scholarship rules differ by programme.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official sources

Applications go to individual Erasmus Mundus course consortia, and scholarship rules differ by programme. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Choose the course, not just the funding

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters are taught by international groups of universities. Begin with the official catalogue and follow the chosen course to its consortium site. Check the degree taught, the universities involved, where the teaching takes place and which language qualifications are required. Different courses can have completely different admission conditions.

Check admission and scholarship separately

An application for a degree does not always guarantee consideration for its scholarships. On the consortium site, look for a dedicated scholarship selection question, funding criteria and the current intake notice. If the wording is unclear, ask the consortium whether a separate funding application is needed before you submit.

Prepare for the mobility requirement

A joint master can involve more than one study country. Compare the schedule, travel requirements, qualification awarded and visa arrangements rather than assuming the first university will be the only destination. Read precisely what financial support covers and plan for any expenses that the award does not pay.

Submit through the correct channel

The European Commission's guidance tells students to apply directly to the institution managing the selected course. Prepare your transcripts, references, study statement and language evidence according to that consortium's instructions. Use the official site for deadlines, not a third-party summary that may describe an earlier intake.

Before submitting your application

Make a copy of the current official call, record the programme name and route, and confirm the time zone of any stated deadline. Check nationality, degree level, language requirements and the documents needed for your particular programme. If the relevant details are missing, contact the provider rather than treating the programme as open or as a guaranteed personal match.

There is no single application form for every Erasmus Mundus master's

The European Commission's Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters programme includes courses organised by different university consortia. Applicants choose a particular course in the official Erasmus Mundus catalogue, then apply directly to the institution or consortium running that master's. The consortium publishes the actual admission criteria, documents and scholarship selection process.

A course being listed in the catalogue does not prove its next intake is accepting applications, and an admission offer does not automatically mean a funded scholarship. Check both items separately on the selected consortium's current website.

Who can apply and what a joint master's involves

Official programme featureWhat the applicant should verify
First degreeA completed bachelor's degree or being in the final undergraduate year with graduation before the master's begins; recognised equivalents may be considered according to national rules
Study durationOne to two academic years, corresponding to 60, 90 or 120 ECTS credits
Joint deliveryParticipating universities, intended mobility schedule and degree award arrangements
International eligibilityApplicants worldwide may apply at programme level, but check the specific consortium's conditions
FundingWhether full scholarships are available for the chosen intake and how selection is carried out

Official Erasmus Mundus guidance says full scholarships can cover participation costs and contribute to travel, visa and living expenses. This does not mean every catalogue programme or every admitted student receives the same scholarship. Read the selected master's financial details, including any extra costs during mobility.

Worked example: comparing two potential courses

Imagine an applicant is interested in public health and data analysis. Course A requires prior coursework in statistics, while Course B requires a language certificate and a different background. Even if both appear in the same Erasmus Mundus catalogue, the applicant should build two separate evidence packs and verify the selection criteria for each course. A generic Erasmus application guide cannot establish eligibility for either.

DecisionQuestion to record
Academic fitDoes the named course accept my degree and modules?
Language fitWhich tests, exemptions and score validity dates does that consortium accept?
MobilityWhich countries will I study in, and will I need multiple visas or residence permits?
FinancingIs scholarship selection part of the same form as admission? What expenses remain?
DeadlineWhat is the current intake, official time zone and separate scholarship deadline, if any?

The European Commission says applications are commonly submitted between October and January for programmes starting the next academic year. Treat that as a typical period, not a deadline for your selected master's. Confirm the precise dates directly on the consortium page.

Checking the 2027 intake

In July 2026, the European Commission announced 37 newly selected Joint Masters that would join the catalogue, with student applications scheduled to open in autumn 2026 for September 2027 starts. This is a programme-level announcement, not proof that any particular course has opened its student portal. Use the 2026 Commission announcement alongside the current catalogue and the individual course website.
