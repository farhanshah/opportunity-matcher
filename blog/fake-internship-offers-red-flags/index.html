Fake Internship Offers: How to Verify Recruiters and Application Links

Use official vacancy references and employer domains to protect applicants from recruitment scams.

OppMatcher editorial · Application guidance23 October 2026

Use official vacancy references and employer domains to protect applicants from recruitment scams. Use this guide to evaluate a specific posting against its official evidence; it is not a guarantee that any particular internship or selection process is currently open.

Start with the official employer site

Search for the position title and reference in the organisation's current career portal. A recruiter can copy a real vacancy description and redirect applicants to an unrelated payment form. Compare the exact URL, not just the logo or organisation name.

Question requests for advance payments

Unexpected application, interview, training or visa processing fees are serious warning signs, especially if demanded by private messaging. Reputable humanitarian organisations publish clear policies about recruitment charges. Do not transfer money to a personal account to secure a placement.

Protect identity and banking information

Early recruitment steps rarely require bank details or passport scans sent through chat. When legitimate employers need personal documents, they should provide secure official procedures. Review sender domains and independently contact the institution if a message looks unusual.

Keep proof before reporting fraud

Save the suspicious email or URL and the exact vacancy advertised. Notify the real employer through a verified channel and use local reporting services where appropriate. Avoid forwarding sensitive attachments widely when asking others to check an offer.

Worked example

Example: someone receives an 'UN internship selection' message from a free email account requesting a refundable training fee. They should find the vacancy in the agency's official system and verify the sender independently before sharing documents.

Before applying

  1. Check the posting's requisition number
  2. Never pay to secure an interview
  3. Use official contact channels for verification

Five warning signs worth checking before you reply

The US Federal Trade Commission's April 2026 warning describes unsolicited messages offering remote jobs, vague tasks, and later requests for money. Its task-scam guidance explains how fake earnings dashboards can be used to convince applicants to deposit their own money.

Warning signalSafer verification step
Unexpected WhatsApp or Telegram job offerFind the employer's official recruitment site independently. Do not rely on a link sent by the recruiter.
Payment required to “activate” work, unlock commission or receive wagesDo not pay to obtain the job or withdraw alleged earnings.
Recruiter uses a lookalike web domainCompare the exact domain against the employer's own careers page, including spelling and subdomains.
High pay for unclear remote tasksAsk for the real job reference, responsibilities, manager, work arrangement and written contract.
Pressure to supply passport, bank details or identity photos immediatelyUse the secure recruitment process only after you independently verify the employer and role.

Worked example: a fake internship sent through chat

Someone receives: “International internship selected. Earn daily pay by boosting products. Reply YES and transfer a small refundable fee to activate.” There is no employer vacancy reference and no application screening. The claimed daily pay and upfront transfer match warning patterns documented by the FTC. The safest response is not to engage or send money, and to verify independently whether the employer posted such a vacancy.

A real employer can legitimately ask for identification later in a secure onboarding process, but an unsolicited message alone is not trustworthy evidence that an offer exists.

University and UN agency names do not establish authenticity

Scammers may copy logos from universities or international organisations. The official UNHCR careers guidance says UNHCR recruitment does not require application or recruitment fees. A message claiming to be from UNHCR that demands fees should not be treated as genuine merely because it contains a real staff member's name or official-looking PDF.

Check an exact vacancy's title, reference, current deadline and application link on the provider's own website. If the provider does not list it, avoid clicking unknown payment portals or uploading documents.

What to do if you already paid or shared information

  1. Stop further payments and contact your bank or payment provider immediately through an independently verified channel.
  2. Change exposed passwords and enable two-factor authentication on affected accounts.
  3. Report the advert, messaging profile and transaction to the relevant local fraud authority and recruitment platform.
  4. Save the URLs, screenshots and transaction evidence without sharing them publicly.
  5. Contact the actual employer's official careers team if you need to report brand impersonation.

In the US, the FTC accepts reports at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Elsewhere use the appropriate local authority. OppMatcher's corrections page can be used to flag a misleading link on our website; it cannot recover stolen funds.
