Global Korea Scholarship (GKS): Graduate and Embassy Routes

Use the official GKS notice to compare degree levels, routes and document rules.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official sources

Use the official GKS notice to compare degree levels, routes and document rules. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Find the right year's announcement

Study in Korea provides government scholarship information, but the rules can differ between undergraduate and graduate intakes. Open the current notice and its application guidelines. Do not use a prior year's document checklist as if it were valid now.

Check the application route

Embassy and university pathways have different submission procedures and restrictions. Determine which is permitted for your nationality and intended programme. Be especially careful about rules that limit the number of institutions or departments you may choose.

Plan language and degree evidence

A university may teach in Korean or English and may impose additional language conditions. Read the current GKS guidance for academic records, graduation proof, citizenship documents and certifications. Allow time for official translations or verification.

Make the programme choice practical

Compare degree curriculum, research fit, city, living conditions and any language training. A scholarship name does not tell you whether the actual department is suitable. Use the university's own course website as a second primary source.

Before submitting your application

Make a copy of the current official call, record the programme name and route, and confirm the time zone of any stated deadline. Check nationality, degree level, language requirements and the documents needed for your particular programme. If the relevant details are missing, contact the provider rather than treating the programme as open or as a guaranteed personal match.

GKS graduate programme: distinguish the academic year from an open application

On 2 February 2026, South Korea's official Study in Korea GKS centre announced the 2026 graduate scholarship guidelines, with a revised set of documents published on 20 February 2026. That announcement describes the 2026 intake and cannot be treated as a currently open 2027 graduate call. As of 3 November 2026, applicants should look for an official, newer intake notice rather than using old deadlines.

The 2026 graduate notice described a planned 2,000 international student invitations. That is the published target for that year's graduate programme, not proof of a current number of funded places, nor a guarantee for any candidate.

Embassy Track vs University Track

The GKS centre identifies two routes for eligible graduate degree applicants. On the Embassy Track, candidates apply through the Korean diplomatic route corresponding to their country of citizenship. On the University Track, candidates send the application to a designated Korean university that carries out the initial selection.

The official Study in Korea scholarship page says eligible Embassy Track applicants may select up to three universities within permitted Type A and B categories, with at least one Type B university for the referenced programme rules. University Track applicants follow their designated institution's criteria. These details can change by year and track.

Documents: the official files are tied to the chosen intake

The GKS 2026 graduate announcement included a detailed application-guidelines archive, updated forms and university-information sets for general and specialised fields. A programme-specific university list matters: a university participating in GKS generally does not mean every degree or department is available under every scholarship track.

Use the current guide to confirm accepted academic qualifications, graduation status, citizenship evidence, family documentation, language certificates and any apostille or consular-authentication rules. Avoid borrowing forms from another academic year or using an unrelated university page as evidence of eligibility.

Worked example: choosing a research-focused programme

A graduate applicant interested in artificial intelligence finds two Korean universities offering similar course titles. University A may be approved for one GKS specialisation, while University B could be eligible only under another track. Before choosing, the applicant should match their citizenship, degree level, intended field and exact participating university against the latest government list. A university's ordinary admission page is not proof that it has scholarship capacity in the relevant track.

Likewise, a language test can support an application but does not automatically satisfy every academic department's language rule. Consult the designated university as well as the general scholarship guidelines before treating a test score as sufficient.

A safe GKS preparation sequence

  1. Open the official GKS announcement and current notices to identify the correct year and degree category.
  2. Choose the Embassy or University Track for which you are eligible.
  3. Confirm the participating universities, eligible departments and selection rules in the correct official university information files.
  4. Check the exact submission route and deadline for the embassy or designated university, including local time zone.
  5. Gather the required original documents and certification evidence for that cycle.
  6. Prepare your own study plan with real course or research reasons, then submit through the specified official route.

GKS undergraduate versus graduate scholarships: choose the right route

The GKS undergraduate and graduate programmes use different qualification levels and can have separate applicant-country quotas, institutional lists and nomination routes. Do not assume that documents and dates from the graduate guide apply to an undergraduate applicant.

Begin at the South Korea scholarship hub and look for the current official GKS announcement appropriate to your degree level. Verify any embassy-track or university-track details and the current translation requirements before requesting documents.
