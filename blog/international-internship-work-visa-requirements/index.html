International Internship Visa and Work Authorisation: What to Ask

Understand the difference between permission to travel and permission to complete a placement.

Understand the difference between permission to travel and permission to complete a placement. Use this guide to evaluate a specific posting against its official evidence; it is not a guarantee that any particular internship or selection process is currently open.

Identify the legal form of the placement

An internship may be treated as employment, training, volunteering or part of an accredited study course depending on local rules. The host organisation should describe the contract and duties accurately. A tourist visa is not automatically permission to perform work.

Ask the receiving institution specific questions

Request the expected start and end dates, paid or unpaid status, work location and any sponsorship documents it can provide. Check the host country's current official immigration guidance for your nationality and circumstances before booking flights.

Include remote cross-border work

Working from a different country can raise employer authorisation, insurance and tax issues even if the organisation calls the internship home-based. Confirm the permitted country of work in writing. Do not infer freedom to work from a virtual interview invitation.

Avoid relying on old social posts

Immigration conditions can change faster than evergreen internship guides. Use government sources for the actual application path and seek qualified immigration advice for complex cases. Keep visa and job requirements separate so you do not mistake an offer for immigration approval.

Worked example

Example: an applicant offered an unpaid placement in the UK should confirm whether the specific activity is allowed under their current immigration permission. The fact that the placement pays no salary is not sufficient evidence of permission.

Before applying

  1. Confirm placement category and location
  2. Use the host government's current rules
  3. Ask for employment and sponsorship documents
