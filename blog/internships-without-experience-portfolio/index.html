Internships Without Experience: Show Skills with Coursework and Projects

Use credible examples of course projects and volunteering when you have no formal work history.

OppMatcher editorial · Application guidancePublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Use credible examples of course projects and volunteering when you have no formal work history. Use this guide to evaluate a specific posting against its official evidence; it is not a guarantee that any particular internship or selection process is currently open.

Start with the vacancy's requirements

Some internships are intended for beginners; others require technical experience or graduate study. Focus on opportunities where your education and skills meet mandatory conditions. Avoid the mistake of interpreting 'entry level' as no qualifications or restrictions.

Translate coursework into real evidence

A laboratory report can support an application for a quality role, while a student marketing campaign can demonstrate planning and communication. Describe the question, action and deliverable. You do not need to claim that a classroom project was a paid client assignment.

Create a small relevant sample

A one-page policy note, spreadsheet model, portfolio case study or short research summary may help if the provider accepts additional evidence. Keep confidential data out of public samples. Prioritise two examples that align with the posted duties rather than a large unrelated portfolio.

Write honest experience statements

Explain what you contributed personally, where you learned it and what you would like to develop. Review your CV, cover letter and application fields for consistent dates and qualifications. A clear beginner profile is more credible than exaggerated job titles.

Worked example

Example: a student applying for a data internship can describe cleaning a course dataset, checking missing values and building a chart. Those are specific skills even if they have never held a data analyst job.

Before applying

  1. Match courses to listed duties
  2. Select two verifiable work samples
  3. Do not describe coursework as employment

Frequently asked question

Can I apply for an internship with no work experience?

Often, yes. Check whether experience is listed as required or only desirable. UNICEF, for example, treats relevant experience as an additional advantage, not a requirement. Show the skills the role needs through coursework, projects and volunteering, with specific results.

Check whether experience is actually required

Many students assume internships demand experience they don't have. Often they don't. UNICEF's internship programme, for example, sets these requirements: you must be at least 18; currently enrolled in an undergraduate, graduate or PhD programme, or have graduated within the past two years; and proficient in English, French or Spanish. It expects strong academic performance and motivation, and describes relevant professional experience as an additional advantage, not a requirement. Internships there run from six to twenty-six weeks.

So the first step is to sort each vacancy's criteria into "required" and "desirable". If experience is only desirable, your job is to show the skills the role needs through other evidence.

Build a small portfolio for the role you want

A portfolio doesn't need to be a website. For most applications, two or three pieces of work that match the vacancy are enough. Choose pieces where:

  • the task resembles what an intern in that team would do, such as a literature summary for a research unit, a data visualisation for an analytics team, or a short communications piece for an outreach team;
  • you can explain your own contribution clearly;
  • nothing confidential or belonging to someone else is included.

Put a short link on your CV, and refer to one piece in your motivation statement. A reviewer is more likely to open one well-labelled link than a list of ten.

Worked example: matching coursework to a data internship

A vacancy for a monitoring and evaluation intern lists "data analysis in Excel or similar" and "clear written reporting" as desirable. The applicant has no work experience but did a statistics module project analysing survey data. They:

  1. rewrite the project as a one-page summary: the question, the dataset (publicly available), the method, three findings and one limitation;
  2. make a single clean chart from it;
  3. add one CV line: "Analysed a public household-survey dataset (2,400 responses) in Excel; summarised findings in a 1-page brief."

That line names the tool, the scale and the output, and every part of it can be checked.

Write the application around the vacancy

Use the vacancy's own wording to structure your statement: take two or three of its listed competencies and give one concrete example for each. Avoid listing personal qualities without evidence. "Hard-working and passionate" tells the reviewer nothing they can check. UNICEF, for instance, asks applicants to review each role's requirements and tailor their profile to them. Only shortlisted candidates are contacted, so a generic application is easy to pass over.

Checklist

  1. Separate required from desirable criteria in each vacancy.
  2. Confirm you meet the enrolment or graduation window.
  3. Pick two or three pieces of work that match the role.
  4. Rewrite each as a short, verifiable summary.
  5. Reference them in both your CV and your statement.
  6. Apply only through the organisation's official portal.
