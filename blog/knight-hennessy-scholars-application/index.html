Knight-Hennessy Scholars: Stanford Application Checklist

Build a separate plan for graduate admission and Knight-Hennessy funding.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 4 November 2026 with official guidance

Build a separate plan for graduate admission and Knight-Hennessy funding. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Plan two independent submissions

Stanford graduate programmes and Knight-Hennessy Scholars assess different things. Confirm the eligible degree first, then create separate submission checklists for the department and scholarship. An excellent funding application cannot replace the university's academic review.

Check the first-degree timing rules

The programme's graduation window matters. Compare the date of your first bachelor's degree with the official eligibility period for the actual cohort. Also review any eligible-degree restrictions and departmental test or portfolio requirements.

Use evidence of real initiative

Choose a project where you solved a problem, brought people together or changed a process. Describe your actions and what you learned, rather than listing every position on your CV. Be accurate about what your team accomplished and what you personally did.

Prepare for differing review stages

A scholarship process may include supplementary evaluation or conversations, while a graduate programme may ask for references and academic evidence. Keep decisions, acceptances and funding steps separate so an outstanding task is not missed.

Before submitting your application

Make a copy of the current official call, record the programme name and route, and confirm the time zone of any stated deadline. Check nationality, degree level, language requirements and the documents needed for your particular programme. If the relevant details are missing, contact the provider rather than treating the programme as open or as a guaranteed personal match.

2027 KHS cohort is closed; 2028 applications open later

Stanford's official Knight-Hennessy Scholars deadline page says the application for the 2027 cohort closed on 6 October 2026 at 1:00 pm Pacific Time. Applications for the 2028 cohort are expected to open in summer 2027. As of 4 November 2026, do not label the KHS 2027 application as open. The degree programme application deadline for someone who already applied to KHS may be different, but that does not reopen the closed KHS application.

Two applications, two admissions decisions

Every incoming Knight-Hennessy scholar must be admitted to an eligible full-time Stanford graduate degree and separately selected by KHS. You cannot replace your department's graduate admission with a scholarship application. Stanford's official KHS preparation resources distinguish the scholarship's leadership assessment from the academic degree programme's application.

The first bachelor's degree date matters

According to KHS's published eligibility criteria for the 2027 cohort, the first bachelor's degree, or international equivalent, must have been earned in January 2020 or later. Certain military applicants have an extended window beginning in January 2018. The deadline is linked to the first bachelor's degree, not a more recent master's qualification. A graduate who received their first degree in 2018 and a master's in 2025 would generally fall outside the normal KHS 2027 window, absent an applicable exception.

Eligibility criteria may change for the next cohort, so do not simply replace 2027 with 2028 and calculate dates yourself. KHS has no general age or nationality restriction, but eligible Stanford graduate admission, the first-degree window and other current programme rules still apply.

KHS explicitly restricts AI-written application responses

Stanford's KHS policy on AI use permits limited brainstorming, organising an applicant's existing ideas, identifying unclear passages and light spelling or grammar edits. It does not permit AI-generated draft answers, sample answers, narrative structures or substantial rewrites that replace the applicant's voice and judgment. Do not copy an example response from a third-party scholarship site into your KHS application.

KHS's official short-answer and essay page describes, for the published cohort, three shorter responses concerning intentions, experiences and surprising personal facts, plus a reflective essay focused on the influences shaping the applicant's identity. The precise prompts and word limits should be read directly on the current page and application portal.

Worked example: planning without generating an essay

A candidate may have independently managed a university project, mentored colleagues and participated in community work. For the KHS application, they can examine which experiences actually illustrate their own decision-making and values. They should write their personal reflection themselves, using real events and results rather than an AI-generated narrative. For Stanford admission, they should separately identify technical coursework or research preparation relevant to the selected graduate programme.

Before the next application round

  1. Confirm the next cohort's published KHS admissions information.
  2. Check the first-degree window and whether your proposed Stanford degree is eligible.
  3. Follow the current degree department's instructions and deadline separately from KHS.
  4. Write any personal essays yourself, following KHS's own AI-writing restrictions.
  5. Use OppMatcher's readiness worksheet only to track factual requirements and outstanding tasks, not to produce submitted responses.
