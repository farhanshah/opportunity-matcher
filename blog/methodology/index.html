Research methodology

How OppMatcher verifies opportunities and application guidance

Our published evidence rules, vacancy freshness checks, deadline handling, nationality restrictions and editorial corrections, with dated examples from actual records.

OppMatcherUpdated 4 November 2026

OppMatcher combines public programme information with exact vacancy records. These are different kinds of evidence. A programme can exist without accepting new applications, and a vacancy can have a deadline without being open to every nationality or degree. We keep those differences visible in our public pages.

1. Which sources do we trust?

Our first choice is the organisation responsible for the award or job: the university, scholarship administrator, employer or government programme. We use the specific current application notice when it is available. A general programme homepage can establish the programme's existence and background rules, but cannot prove a particular intake is open.

2. What qualifies as a recently verified open vacancy?

Our dedicated Verified Opportunities directory uses a stricter filter than the general programme catalogue. At the time of this methodology update, the published software checks that a specific record is marked open and status-verified, has exact-level verification, a meaningful vacancy title, a recognised numeric provider job identifier, a valid HTTPS official-source link and a future deadline. Its last successful check must also be within the previous seven days. These automated criteria are implemented in the public opportunity endpoint, not just written as an editorial aspiration.

Seven days is a maximum evidence age for that public directory. It is not a guarantee that the employer has not changed the vacancy in the intervening hours. Every listing therefore provides its last-check date and the official posting link. If a record fails the current checks, it should not continue to be promoted as an open, exact-verified vacancy.

Why some opportunities disappear from the active directoryWe prefer an incomplete live directory to invented openings. A source that is blocked, stale, unnamed or missing a valid deadline is not a confirmed open individual vacancy. Other provider programme pages may still appear in the broader discovery experience with an uncertainty label.

3. What happens when a deadline passes?

Expired and previously verified vacancy URLs can remain accessible as historical records. Their pages should show Closed when the stored deadline has passed or the provider status is closed. If a posting is not recently confirmed and is not definitively closed, its status should be Verification needed. Neither state is treated as an open application. Active vacancy sitemap URLs are generated only from current qualifying listings; an expired vacancy can therefore remain readable to people with an old link without being advertised as accepting applications.

Historical records may contain old application links, and providers can take their pages down. The official portal remains the source of truth. A closed-page archive is for research, not for submitting an application.

4. How do we handle nationality and other eligibility restrictions?

We preserve restrictions explicitly stated by the official vacancy. For instance, our 3 November 2026 recorded UNICEF China Communications Internship (reference 596670) explicitly limited applicants to Chinese nationals. Another UNICEF Indonesia notice (reference 596461) specified Indonesian applicants living with disabilities. Those examples are based on the titles and recorded source evidence of particular vacancies, not general eligibility assumptions about UNICEF's entire internship programme.

We do not assume eligibility is unrestricted when no condition appears in a job title. Requirements for residence, language, degree equivalence and work authorisation can still exist in the full notice. If a detail is not supported by suitably precise official evidence, we leave it unconfirmed and direct the applicant to the provider.

This is a dated illustration of our screening approach, not an up-to-the-minute opening announcement. The Senegal vacancy can expire quickly, and neither entry proves personal eligibility. Our public pages need to update automatically when their deadline or verification state changes.

6. What makes an application guide ready for search?

We aim for original practical guidance: real differences between named programmes, concrete examples or comparison tools, transparent links to official rules, and accurate time-sensitive statements. Repeating a generic checklist across many pages is not enough. Where an article is too short or its sources are insufficient, we may temporarily remove it from search indexing and AdSense inventory while it is improved. The existing link can remain available with an editorial notice and a link to better-supported guidance.

Adding an article to a sitemap does not guarantee Google will index it. Likewise, the presence of an AdSense script does not mean that the page meets Google's content policies. Editorial quality and actual user usefulness take precedence over the total number of pages.

7. How can a reader correct our information?

Use the Contact & Corrections page to report the OppMatcher URL, the official source URL, the specific claim and the new evidence. A newly published provider deadline or an explicit nationality restriction should take precedence over our older summary. Please avoid emailing passports, full application forms or bank documents when reporting an error.

Because some official sites block automatic fetching, source monitoring can fail or become stale. We prefer to show that uncertainty instead of giving a false Open label. We also avoid promising application acceptance, visa eligibility, funding amounts or processing times that the provider has not confirmed.

Our rule in one sentenceOppMatcher helps you discover and compare opportunities. The organisation offering the opportunity decides the current status, eligibility, funding and application outcome.
