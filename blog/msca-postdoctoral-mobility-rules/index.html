MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships: Mobility Rules and Host Planning

Read doctoral-status, mobility and host conditions before designing an MSCA proposal.

Confirm your research stage

Marie Skłodowska-Curie postdoctoral actions are designed for researchers who have completed a doctorate under the relevant eligibility conditions. Someone beginning PhD study should look for doctoral networks or other schemes instead of assuming the name covers all research careers.

Understand mobility as a rule

Eligibility may depend on where the researcher lived or conducted their main activity during a specified reference period. Travel history, remote work and earlier employment can be relevant. Use the current work programme and official guide for the exact country and time test.

Work with a committed host

Identify a research group with the facilities and supervision required. The host organisation takes responsibility for parts of the application and project delivery. Begin contact early enough to discuss a credible training plan, institutional approvals and the practical transfer of knowledge.

Design more than a research abstract

Explain the problem, methods, training, career-development value and realistic outputs. Show what skills the host offers that you cannot easily acquire alone. The proposal also needs practical delivery steps and may involve secondments depending on the call.

Practical example

Example: a researcher who has spent much of the reference period working in a proposed host country should verify the mobility condition before spending weeks on a project proposal.

What to check

  1. Check doctoral completion criteria
  2. Calculate mobility against the official reference period
  3. Secure host research-office support

MSCA mobility rule: 12 months in the previous 36

The European Commission's official MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships guidance states that researchers must not have lived or carried out their main activity (such as work or study) in the relevant country for more than 12 months during the 36 months immediately before the application deadline. The relevant country differs by fellowship type: the beneficiary's country for European Fellowships and the outgoing-phase partner's country for Global Fellowships.

For example, if an applicant spent 15 months studying in the proposed European Fellowship host country during the relevant 36-month window, that normally exceeds the mobility threshold, even if they then moved elsewhere. An applicant who spent 10 months there may satisfy this particular rule, but must still meet the other conditions. Use actual dates and consult official guidance for exceptions, including permitted short visits, rather than treating these illustrative cases as an eligibility decision.

Doctoral degree and research-experience rules

Applicants generally need a PhD or a successfully defended doctoral thesis by the call deadline and must satisfy a limit of eight full-time-equivalent years of postdoctoral research experience, with certain excluded periods or exceptions. These are separate from the mobility rule: being eligible on one does not prove the other. Prepare exact dates for the PhD, research activity, career breaks, residence and employment.

Frequently asked mobility questions

Does remote employment count as main activity?

The guidance refers to residence and main activity including work or study. Remote employment location can therefore matter. If your residence and contracting country differ, document the facts and seek clarification from the host or national contact point.

Is 12 months or 13 months in the host country allowed?

The standard rule prohibits more than 12 months during the 36-month reference period. Twelve months can meet that particular maximum, whereas 13 months generally exceeds it, subject to how the current call defines and counts eligible dates.
