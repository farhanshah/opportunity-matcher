Paid vs Unpaid Internships Abroad: Total Cost and Career Value

Compare compensation, location expenses and learning outcomes rather than relying on the internship label.

OppMatcher editorial · Application guidancePublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Compare compensation, location expenses and learning outcomes rather than relying on the internship label. Use this guide to evaluate a specific posting against its official evidence; it is not a guarantee that any particular internship or selection process is currently open.

Calculate the true placement cost

Include rent, food, transport, insurance, visa charges and initial deposits for an on-site placement. If the employer offers a stipend, find out whether it is paid monthly, after completion or by reimbursement. The stated compensation can be insufficient in an expensive city.

Distinguish stipend from wages

Some internships provide an allowance while others pay wages under a local employment contract. Benefits, taxes and working hours can differ. Never assume an allowance covers healthcare or relocation. Read the written terms and check the official organisation guidance.

Evaluate the work you will actually learn

Ask what deliverables interns produce, who supervises them, whether their work is reviewed and whether the role develops relevant skills. A short unpaid research placement with a clear mentor may suit a student's goals, while a long unpaid role with no learning plan may be a poor choice.

Set a personal decision threshold

Choose an affordable maximum total cost and minimum quality requirements before applying. Avoid getting attached to a famous organisation when the placement would require funding you do not possess. Keep the host's exact conditions separate from your own budget estimates.

Worked example

Example: compare a remote stipend internship with an unpaid six-month on-site role. Calculate net accommodation, transport and visa expenses for both, and write down specific outputs or skills each role offers.

Before applying

  1. Estimate complete costs, not stipend alone
  2. Check length and supervision
  3. Confirm written terms before accepting

Frequently asked question

Is an unpaid internship legal in the UK?

It depends on whether you count as a worker. If you do, you are normally owed the National Minimum Wage, whatever the role is called. The main exceptions in UK government guidance are placements of under a year that are required by a UK course, genuine voluntary work for charities and similar bodies, and work shadowing with no work done.

"Intern" is not a legal category, at least in the UK

The UK government's guidance on employment rights and pay for interns is blunt: words like internship, work placement and work experience have no legal status on their own. What matters is whether you count as a worker. If you do, you are normally due the National Minimum Wage, whatever the role is called. HMRC's own manual says the same: calling someone an intern has no bearing on their minimum wage entitlement.

In practice, you are likely to be a worker, and owed the minimum wage, if you have a contract or arrangement to do work (even a verbal one), you are required to turn up, the organisation has to have work for you, or you are promised a contract of future work. The main exceptions in the government guidance are:

  • students required to do an internship of less than one year as part of a UK-based further or higher education course;
  • school work-experience placements;
  • genuine volunteers and voluntary workers for charities and similar organisations;
  • people only shadowing, with no work to do.

If an unpaid intern claims arrears, it is the employer who must prove the person was not a worker. Other countries use different rules, so check the labour law of the country where you will be working.

International organisations: stipend policies vary by agency

UN-system internships show how far "unpaid" and "paid" can differ even within one family of organisations:

OrganisationWhat the official source says
UN SecretariatInterns are not paid. Travel, insurance, accommodation and living costs fall to the intern or a sponsoring institution, as do visas.
UNICEFA monthly stipend, plus a one-time lump sum towards travel and visa costs when funding is available
UNDPUnder its internship policy (updated 2020), a monthly stipend whose rate depends on the duty station. Remote interns receive a reduced remote rate.
UNESCOCheck the current call. OppMatcher's catalogue lists the UNESCO Internship Programme as unpaid.

A stipend is not a salary. It is meant to contribute to living costs, and the rate is set per location. Always read the amount and conditions in the specific vacancy or agreement letter.

Work out the real cost before accepting

Whether a placement is "worth it" unpaid depends on what it will cost you to do it, not on the organisation's reputation. List:

  1. rent for the full period, including any deposit;
  2. food, local transport and phone;
  3. return travel;
  4. visa and health insurance;
  5. income you give up from a job you would otherwise do.

Worked example: three months in an expensive city

Suppose a 12-week internship in a city where a room costs €900 a month, with €500 a month for food and transport, €600 for flights and €250 for visa and insurance.

  • Living: 3 × (€900 + €500) = €4,200
  • Travel, visa and insurance: €600 + €250 = €850
  • Total: €5,050

An unpaid placement means finding all €5,050. A placement with a €1,000 monthly stipend reduces the gap to €2,050, and one with a one-off travel contribution reduces it further. A paid role closer to home might cost nothing and pay you. These figures are placeholders: replace them with real rents and the stipend in your agreement letter.

Judging value beyond pay

If you are considering an unpaid or low-stipend role, ask specific questions:

  • Will I have a named supervisor and defined tasks, or general support work?
  • Can I get academic credit, which may also change my legal status?
  • Is the placement remote? It may cost far less than one in person.
  • Are there funds I can apply for, such as university travel bursaries or departmental grants?
  • Will I be allowed to apply for other positions during or after the internship?

Never pay a fee to an employer to be "placed" in an internship with a UN agency or government body. Those organisations recruit through their own portals. See our guide to fake internship offers.
