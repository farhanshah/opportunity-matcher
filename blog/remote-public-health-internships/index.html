Remote Public Health Internships: Roles and Eligibility

Compare official programme descriptions, practical tasks and the location restrictions that matter before applying.

OppMatcher editorial · Official programme researchReviewed 16 November 2026
Start with the kind of work, then check eligibility. Remote public health internships can involve health education, evaluation, policy research, communications and nonprofit programme support. They are not always clinical placements, paid jobs or work-from-anywhere opportunities. We compared published policies from three organisations to show what the labels really mean.

What can a remote public health intern actually work on?

Public health covers programmes, evidence and population health, not only patient-facing work. A student interested in epidemiology might look for research and survey work. Someone from a communications background may be useful in health education. Policy students may support legislative monitoring or briefing notes, while administration students may support events and programme operations.

For a remote position, look for deliverables you can reasonably complete off-site: literature reviews, spreadsheet analysis, report drafting, policy summaries, communication materials or online stakeholder coordination. Ask whether the team expects direct community or patient contact. Some remote programmes cannot offer that experience.

SOPHE: remote health education without patient contact

SOPHE's official internship page says it offers fully remote unpaid work. Tasks can include continuing education, coalition support, research and advocacy assistance. The organisation says the placement is office-based and does not offer direct patient or community interaction. That distinction is especially important for a student whose degree requires hands-on clinical or field placement.

The listed requirements include being enrolled or recently graduated, generally having completed two or more years of coursework with a specified academic standard, interest in health promotion or policy, and computer and writing skills. The website also describes required documents such as a résumé, cover letter and writing sample. Check the current SOPHE page for the correct intake instructions rather than copying dates from an archived academic term.

NRHA: remote communications and rural-health programmes

NRHA's internship page distinguishes its teams. Communications work can involve evaluating rural-health news, editing content and monitoring information. Program Services may involve research, health equity, event and nonprofit-management tasks. Its foundation-development concentration is described as fully remote and focuses on fundraising and impact reporting.

The official page also says interns generally work around 16–24 hours per week, depending on the team and schedule. This is a programme description, not a guarantee that a particular applicant can start immediately or work from any country. Before applying, check the concentration, whether its application is open, and the actual legal and scheduling arrangements.

IMR: why the country restriction matters

International Medical Relief's public health internship page is useful because it explicitly says it seeks remote interns across the 50 United States. The role is project-based, with options spanning health and nonclinical operational work. A student living outside the United States should not take that wording as worldwide eligibility simply because the organisation works internationally.

If your home country is outside the advertised area, contact the official recruitment team about eligibility only if the notice permits enquiries. Do not state a different residence or use an unrelated employment location to bypass the rule. An older programme page also cannot confirm a current deadline.

How to choose a relevant remote public health internship

  1. Decide the skill you need to practise. Choose health communications, policy, evaluation, programme operations or research; each has different tasks and evidence requirements.
  2. Check the exact working location. “Remote within the United States” differs from a globally remote role. The work may be online but the contract geography is still restricted.
  3. Confirm academic fit. Look for degree level, coursework completed, grade requirements, specific skills and whether the university awards credit.
  4. Read the payment terms. Some nonprofit internships are explicitly unpaid. Ask about equipment, internet, training and any expected hours before accepting.
  5. Find the current official form. A programme landing page may show old semester dates. The current advertised application route is the source of truth.
  6. Prepare a relevant sample. For health communications, show a concise accurate educational brief. For evaluation work, show a clearly labelled example of survey analysis with appropriately anonymised or synthetic data.
  7. Check safeguarding and confidentiality. A remote intern should not receive confidential health records or patient data through insecure personal channels.

Worked example: making a useful application without prior public health employment

Imagine a public policy student who completed a university project comparing vaccination information campaigns. For a health communication role, they can describe the project's audience, what public data they reviewed, how they checked the accuracy of claims and what they wrote or designed. For a data evaluation placement, they should instead emphasise how they cleaned and summarised the dataset and explain its limitations. The point is to offer verifiable examples of relevant skills without claiming to have run a professional health programme.

That example does not replace the vacancy's formal entry rules. A supervisor may require a public health degree, specific tools or local work eligibility regardless of a candidate's enthusiasm.

Frequently asked questions

Can international students apply to remote public health internships?

Sometimes. A provider may accept students abroad, while another may restrict remote work to one country. The IMR example limits its stated remote geography to the 50 US states. Check permitted residence, student status, insurance and any work-permission conditions directly with the provider.

Are remote public health internships always paid?

No. SOPHE and NRHA describe unpaid internship options. Never assume a salary because an advert appears on a commercial job board. Read the organisation's current written terms.

Can a remote public health internship count toward my degree?

That depends on your university. Ask whether your course requires in-person field hours, specific supervision or direct community activity. A remote nonprofit desk role may not satisfy a clinical or practicum requirement.

Do I need an MPH or public health degree?

Not universally. SOPHE also describes related backgrounds including communications and marketing, though programme-specific requirements still apply. Start with relevant skills and confirm the exact education condition in each call.

Where can I check official opportunities?

Review SOPHE, NRHA, IMR, or the Public Health Careers internship directory. For a broader international search, use our remote internship category and validate each provider link.
