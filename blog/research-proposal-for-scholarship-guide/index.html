How to Write a Research Proposal for a Scholarship

Turn a broad research interest into a feasible question with methods and a clear host fit.

OppMatcher editorial · Evidence-first applications23 October 2026
Editorial reviewThis shorter guide is being reviewed for more detailed evidence. For updated, source-backed information, read our related guide and confirm any application decision at the official provider. Do not treat an older deadline or eligibility summary here as a current open call.

Turn a broad research interest into a feasible question with methods and a clear host fit. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Formulate a researchable question

A topic like climate change or artificial intelligence is too broad to show a realistic project. Identify a population, place, dataset or mechanism, and write a question that evidence could answer. Define why the problem is not already settled in the literature.

Choose methods you can carry out

Describe how you will collect or analyse evidence, what resources you need and what limitations you expect. A quantitative proposal should justify its data and analytic approach; a qualitative project should explain recruitment and ethical safeguards where relevant.

Explain why this institution fits

Name relevant facilities, research themes or academic expertise only if they appear in current official information. Avoid inventing a supervisor agreement. State what you would gain from the host and how you could contribute to its work.

Make the timeline and outputs plausible

Break the project into preparation, data collection, analysis and writing stages, with contingency for access or approvals. Specify a realistic output such as a thesis chapter, working paper or dataset. Fit the proposal to the provider's length and formatting instructions.

Practical example

Example: instead of 'I will study food security', propose an analysis of how storage conditions affect postharvest losses for a defined crop, location and measurement period, with a plan for finding usable data.

What to check

  1. Write one researchable question
  2. Verify methods and data access
  3. Align timeline with award length
