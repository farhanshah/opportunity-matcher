Scholarship application documents and when to prepare them

A good application pack is not just a folder of files. It is a set of evidence that matches the exact scholarship and university requirements, in the format and timing they ask for.

Opportunity Research TeamUpdated 15 October 2026Official guidance reviewed 15 October 20267 minute read
Quick answerPrepare identity, academic, experience and writing documents early, but do not upload the same generic file everywhere. Recheck names, dates, formats, page limits and programme specific instructions before each submission.

1. Passport or identity evidence

Keep a clear scan of the identity page of your passport or the document the provider accepts. Check expiry dates early, especially if the scholarship or university requires the document to remain valid through enrolment or visa processing.

Your name should be consistent across the application, degree documents and passport. If there is a genuine difference, follow the provider's guidance for explaining it.

2. Transcripts and degree certificates

Most postgraduate applications need academic transcripts and evidence of the qualification you completed or are completing. Some providers accept unofficial copies at the first stage, while others require certified documents.

Keep the original language version and, when required, an accepted translation. Do not assume a translation standard. The university or scholarship provider decides what certification is acceptable.

3. CV or resume

Your scholarship CV should make eligibility easy to verify. Use clear dates for education, employment, internships, volunteering, leadership and relevant projects. Avoid decorative layouts that make dates or responsibilities difficult to scan.

If work experience is an eligibility rule, keep the dates and hours consistent with the application form. A polished CV cannot compensate for conflicting information.

4. Personal statement, motivation letter or essays

Different programmes ask different questions. One may focus on leadership, another on academic goals, another on development impact or future career plans. Reusing one generic statement without answering the actual prompt weakens the application.

Start from evidence: what you did, what changed, what you learned and why the specific programme fits the next step. Then edit to the provider's word or character limit.

5. References and recommendation letters

References often take longer than applicants expect because another person controls the timing. Ask early and give the referee the scholarship name, deadline, submission method and the kind of evidence the programme values.

Some portals email the referee directly. Others ask you to upload a signed letter. Confirm the process before requesting anything.

6. English or other language evidence

Language evidence can include a recognised test, previous study in the relevant language, or another form accepted by the university. Scholarship and university rules may differ, so check both.

If a test is required, work backwards from score release times and the final application date. A test booking can become an earlier practical deadline.

7. Work experience and activity evidence

Where experience matters, keep employment letters, contracts, payslips or other evidence that the provider may request. The exact evidence differs by programme. Do not upload extra documents simply because you have them.

If the form asks for hours, dates or responsibilities, make sure those details agree with your CV and any evidence you later provide.

8. Programme specific documents

Some opportunities require a research proposal, portfolio, writing sample, nomination form, employer endorsement, course offer, financial statement or medical document. This is why the provider checklist should control your application pack.

File names and version control

Use simple file names that tell you what the document is and which application it belongs to. Keep a clean final version for each scholarship rather than repeatedly overwriting one master file.

Before uploading, check file type, size, page limit, orientation and whether signatures or certification are required.

Final document check before submission

  1. Open the current provider checklist.
  2. Confirm every required file is present.
  3. Check that names and dates agree across documents.
  4. Check word limits and page limits.
  5. Open each PDF after export to make sure it renders correctly.
  6. Confirm reference submissions separately.
  7. Save proof of submission when the provider gives it.
