Scholarship CV Template for International Students 2027

A practical academic CV layout

What the application asks you to show

A scholarship CV is an evidence document. It is designed for an admissions or award committee deciding whether you meet stated criteria, not a generic online résumé. The sections that matter depend on whether the award values academic performance, research, leadership or professional contribution.

How to structure the evidence

A straightforward two-page structure begins with your name and reliable contact details, followed by education, relevant research or academic projects, work or volunteering, publications or awards where genuine, and technical or language evidence. Put the strongest qualifying experience nearest the top. If the provider requests a one-page CV, reduce weaker material instead of shrinking the font.

An example to adapt truthfully

Example entry: University research project, September–December 2025. Analysed publicly available survey data using spreadsheet models; wrote a methods summary and presented two limitations to a class audience. This is an illustrative sentence, not an achievement you should copy if it is untrue.

Before you submit

Check the scholarship's official format and number-of-pages rules. A standard academic CV differs from a Europass form or a university portal's fixed résumé field. Do not invent publications, inflate involvement, or list training that cannot be verified.

  1. Reopen the actual provider's current application requirements.
  2. Check nationality, academic stage and work permission where applicable.
  3. Use only verifiable examples and follow the document format.

A section-by-section template

SectionWhat to includeTip
Personal detailsName, email, phone, nationality if the scholarship is limited by citizenshipLeave out anything the funder has not asked for
EducationDegrees with institution, dates, final or current grade and any distinctionExplain the grading scale if it is not widely known
Research and projectsThesis, publications, conference posters, research assistanceGive the title and your own role
Professional experienceJobs with dates, employer and two or three results eachMany government scholarships check work experience, so make dates exact
Leadership and serviceRoles where you organised people, started something or represented a groupShow what changed because of you
Awards, languages, skillsPrizes, test scores, languages with level, technical skillsOnly list a language test you can document

Make the CV match the rest of the application

Scholarship panels read your CV alongside essays and references. Dates, job titles and results should be identical across all three. If an essay says you led a team of five, the CV should show that role with the same dates.

  1. Check the required format. Some schemes ask for a CV upload with a page limit; others ask you to type your history into the online form instead. Follow the funder's instructions.
  2. Put the most relevant section first. A research scholarship should open with education and research; a leadership scholarship can bring experience and leadership forward.
  3. Count work experience carefully. Where a scheme sets a minimum amount, list start and end dates for each job so the panel can see you meet it.
  4. Remove gaps in logic, not in time. A career break is fine; explain it in one line if it matters.

Example: A hypothetical engineer applying for a master's scholarship has four years of work, one conference paper and a volunteer role teaching maths. Putting work first, with the paper under research and the volunteering under leadership, gives the panel the three things most scholarships look for in two pages.
