Who is eligible for scholarships? 10 requirements explained

Scholarship eligibility rules vary by provider. Use these 10 checks to find out whether you qualify before spending time on essays, documents and references.

OppMatcherUpdated 22 October 2026Official programme guidance checked 22 October 2026
Quick answerThere is no single eligibility rule for every scholarship. Usually you need to check citizenship and residence, education level, academic results, course restrictions, work experience (if required), language evidence, the application route and the deadline. A scholarship can have different rules from the university course it funds.

Scholarship eligibility requirements at a glance

Start with the rules most likely to exclude an application. For each condition, record what the official provider says, whether you meet it and what evidence would prove it. These are common checks, not requirements that apply to every scholarship.

Do not treat a programme's general eligibility page as proof you qualify for a specific course or scholarship round. Check the official application portal and course entry requirements as well.

1. Nationality and citizenship

Many scholarships are open worldwide, but others are limited to citizens of named countries or regions. Read the exact wording. "Citizen of" is different from "resident in", and some programmes require both.

If you hold more than one nationality, check whether the provider asks you to apply through a specific country route.

2. Current and permanent residence

Residence rules are easy to misread. A scholarship may require permanent residence in an eligible country, current residence outside the destination country, or application through a home country nominating body.

Your current visa does not automatically define your permanent residence. Use the provider's own definition and supply evidence if the application asks for it.

3. Degree level and graduation timing

Confirm the minimum qualification and when it must have been completed. Some programmes accept final year students. Others require the degree to be completed before the scholarship application or before the funded course starts.

Also check whether a previous master's degree affects eligibility for another master's scholarship.

4. Academic standard and equivalency

Providers may use degree classifications, GPA thresholds, percentages or country specific equivalency rules. Do not convert your result informally and assume it passes. If the guidance says equivalency is applicant specific, treat it as a check that needs evidence.

5. Field of study and course restrictions

Some scholarships accept any academic field. Others support only named subject areas, development themes, research disciplines or specific university courses.

Check the scholarship rule and the university course entry requirements separately. Passing one does not guarantee the other.

6. Work experience

If a programme requires work experience, confirm the amount, the timing and what counts. The provider may define eligible hours, full time equivalence, paid or unpaid work, internships, volunteering, or work completed after a certain degree.

Keep employment dates and average weekly hours ready so you can calculate experience consistently.

7. English or other language requirements

A scholarship may have no universal language test requirement while the university still requires IELTS, TOEFL or another form of evidence. Keep scholarship eligibility and university admission rules separate.

If your previous degree was taught in English, check whether the university accepts that evidence and what document it needs.

8. Course admission, nomination and application route

Some scholarships need a university application, a nomination, an employer endorsement or a country agency step in addition to the scholarship form. Missing the route can make an otherwise eligible application unusable.

Record who receives each application and whether the steps happen before, after or alongside each other.

9. Return home and other award conditions

Read conditions that apply after selection as carefully as the entry rules. Some awards require recipients to return to their home country for a period after study. Others restrict previous funding, employment relationships or simultaneous awards.

10. Deadline and cycle status

Confirm that you are reading the current cycle. Scholarship pages from previous years can remain indexed in search engines after the application has closed. Use the current provider page and record the date and time zone.

Best practiceWhen a rule is ambiguous, mark it for review instead of calling it a pass or fail. This keeps your shortlist useful without pretending that every provider rule can be automated. Read our research methodology.

Common scholarship eligibility questions

Can I apply for a scholarship in my final year of university?

Sometimes. Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters, for example, can accept final-year bachelor's students who graduate before the programme begins. Other awards require you to complete your degree earlier. Check the completion date the provider uses, not just whether you expect to graduate soon.

Is there a minimum GPA for every scholarship?

No single GPA threshold applies to all scholarships. Some programmes publish a minimum grade, while others assess degree equivalency or academic performance as part of selection. Read both the scholarship and university rules before converting your grades.

Do all scholarships require work experience or an IELTS score?

No. Chevening has an explicit post-undergraduate work experience requirement, but requirements vary widely. Language evidence can also be set by the university separately from the scholarship. Check the specific programme before paying for a test or ruling yourself out.
