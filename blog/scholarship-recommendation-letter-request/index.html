How to Request a Scholarship Recommendation Letter

Give a referee the context, deadlines and supporting facts they need to write an accurate letter.

OppMatcher editorial · Research and application guidePublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Give a referee the context, deadlines and supporting facts they need to write an accurate letter. The goal is to check the exact rules of the programme you intend to use, not to treat any general guide as a confirmed open application.

Choose a referee who knows your work

A famous title is less useful than a person who can discuss your academic progress, project work or leadership with real examples. Check the scholarship's rule for academic versus professional referees before approaching someone.

Make the request specific

State the award, intended course, closing date, submission method and reasons you have chosen this referee. Attach a current CV and a short description of relevant work you completed with them. Avoid asking someone to sign a letter you wrote under their name.

Explain the submission mechanics

Some portals email a unique upload link directly to the referee. Other schemes ask for a letter on institutional stationery. Confirm which process applies and whether the reference is confidential. Send your referee clear instructions, including whether the deadline is in a foreign time zone.

Follow up with reasonable notice

Send a reminder before the internal deadline, not ten messages on the closing day. Thank the referee after submission and confirm the portal received the letter. If a reference becomes impossible, contact the programme about the approved alternative rather than uploading a fabricated one.

Worked example

Example request: 'I'm applying for the Master's funding programme at [university]. Could you describe my research methods and contribution to our final-year project? The referee portal closes on [date/time], and I can provide the project report and CV.'

Action checklist

  1. Confirm referee category
  2. Share the exact prompt and secure submission instructions
  3. Track upload confirmation separately from your application

Frequently asked question

Can my relative write the reference?

Most academic programmes expect independent referees. Check the scheme's conflict-of-interest rule.

Reference rules differ more than applicants expect

Before asking anyone, find out exactly what your scheme needs: how many references, of which type, and when. Three well-known schemes show how much this varies:

SchemeHow many and what typeWhen they're needed
CheveningTwo referees: two professional, two academic, or one of each. Not relatives or close personal friends.You give names and contact details when you apply. Reference letters are needed if you are invited to interview, by Chevening's deadline for that stage.
Gates CambridgeCambridge's funding guidance lists two academic references plus a separate Gates Cambridge reference addressing the scholarship's criteriaReferees submit through the referee portal by the course's funding deadline
Commonwealth Scholarships (CSC)References, plus a supporting statement from your proposed supervisor for PhD applicationsThe CSC says these must be uploaded to the portal before you submit your application

Two practical consequences. First, for the CSC a missing reference can stop you submitting at all, so your referees' timing becomes your deadline. Second, Chevening has said it does not contact referees to chase references: it is the applicant's responsibility to collect them.

Choosing referees

Pick people who have seen your work directly and recently, and who can comment on what the scheme assesses. Chevening's guidance asks referees to cover how long they have known you, in what capacity, and when they last had regular contact with you. A lecturer who supervised your thesis is usually better than a famous professor who taught you in a lecture of 300. A line manager who saw you lead a project is better than a senior executive you met once.

If the scheme asks for an academic reference and you graduated years ago, contact your former supervisor early. Send them your transcript and thesis summary so they can write specifically.

How to ask: a request email

Ask at least four to six weeks before the reference is due, and make it easy to say yes or no:

Subject: Reference request – Chevening Scholarship 2027/28
Dear Dr Okafor,
I'm applying for a Chevening Scholarship to study an MSc in Public Health at [university], starting in September 2027. Would you be willing to act as one of my two referees? You supervised my final-year project on maternal health clinics (2022–23), and I'd value your view of my research and analytical work.
If I'm invited to interview, the reference letter would be needed by [deadline from Chevening's current timeline]. I've attached my CV, a one-page summary of my course choices and career plan, and Chevening's guidance for referees.
I completely understand if you can't take this on. Please let me know either way by [date].
With thanks,
[Name]

Brief your referee, but don't write the letter

Give your referee material to work from: the scheme's criteria, your CV, the course and two or three specific pieces of work they saw you do, with dates. That helps them write concretely. Don't draft the letter for them. Many schemes expect references to be the referee's own words, and some systems require referees to submit directly through their own portal link. A letter that reads like your own essay can harm your credibility.

Worked example: building a reference timeline backwards

An applicant is applying to the CSC, which needs references uploaded before submission. If the CSC deadline is 20 October:

  • Early September: confirm both referees and the supervisor, and send them briefing packs.
  • Late September: send a polite reminder with the portal instructions.
  • 10 October: check the portal. Are both references uploaded? If not, contact the referee directly.
  • 20 October: submit, with a margin for problems.

A referee on leave in October could otherwise cost a whole application cycle.

Reference checklist

  1. Confirm the number, type and deadline of references from the live scheme guidance.
  2. Choose referees who know your work directly. No relatives or close friends.
  3. Ask early, with a briefing pack and a clear deadline.
  4. Check the portal before the deadline, not on it.
  5. Thank your referees and tell them the outcome.
