Scholarship recommendation letter and referee checklist

Help referees provide reliable evidence

How to approach it

A referee should describe direct observations of the candidate's academic, professional or research work. Specific methods, responsibilities and examples are more useful than broad praise.

Share the award criteria, actual deadline and a short list of projects the referee can verify. Do not write an invented letter for someone to sign as their own assessment.

Check whether the referee must submit through a provider portal, use an institutional email, or upload an explicitly signed document. Submission requirements vary by programme.

What makes a recommendation useful?

A strong referee letter explains how the writer knows the applicant and describes work they actually observed. It can distinguish independent work from group contributions. It should not repeat claims from the applicant's CV if the referee cannot verify them. For example, “excellent student” tells the selection panel very little. “I supervised her twelve-week project and observed how she compared two sample preparation methods” identifies a direct relationship and concrete behaviour.

Example evidence a referee can use

Weak claimStronger evidence-based version
“The applicant is a natural leader.”Describe a specific occasion when the applicant organised a task, handled a disagreement or supported a team.
“Their research is exceptional.”Identify the project, the method, the applicant's own contribution and a limitation they recognised.
“They are the best candidate.”Explain the reference group and period, but only if the referee can honestly make that comparison.
“They meet every scholarship requirement.”Address the criteria the writer has direct evidence for; leave nationality and application rules to the official instructions.

A lecturer may be best placed to describe research methods, coursework or academic independence. An internship supervisor may have better evidence of communication, punctuality or applied problem-solving. Choose the person whose observation matches the award's criteria, not simply the most senior person you know.

A practical referee request

Contact a referee before listing their address in an online portal. State the programme name, why you are requesting their reference, the deadline with time zone, the expected format and how the provider will contact them. Give the referee the current award criteria and a concise reminder of your shared work. Make it easy to decline if they cannot write a specific, supportive letter.

“Would you be comfortable providing an academic reference for my application to [programme]? You supervised my [real project or course] in [term], where I worked on [specific task]. The provider asks for a reference submitted through [official process] by [confirmed date and time zone]. I can send the criteria and a short factual summary of that work.”

This is a request template, not a letter for the referee to sign. The referee should prepare and submit their own assessment. Do not invent the applicant's grades, fabricate signatures or send the letter from an account that impersonates the writer.

Check the submission route before you invite anyone

  1. Confirm the required number of referees and whether they must be academic, professional or both.
  2. Check whether the provider sends each referee a unique portal link or accepts signed PDFs. These methods are not interchangeable.
  3. Check if institutional email addresses, letterhead or a signed form are required. Only follow rules stated by that specific provider.
  4. Ask early enough for the referee to review the criteria, but don't pressure them to submit a statement they cannot support.
  5. After submission, check the official portal for completion instead of asking a referee to forward confidential content unless the programme explicitly permits this.

If an award requests only academic references and your supervisor has never taught or assessed you, that relationship may not satisfy the requirement. If a reference must discuss professional experience, explain the tasks the supervisor observed rather than claiming they can evaluate your grades.

Practical checklist

  1. Open the named official provider's current application instructions.
  2. Confirm nationality, residence, degree and other hard rules.
  3. Record supporting documents, time zone and submission process.
