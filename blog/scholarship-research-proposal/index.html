Scholarship Research Proposal Template: Questions and Methods

Build a feasible research plan

What the application asks you to show

A research proposal is a plan for investigating a specific problem. Different scholarship schemes request different lengths, headings and ethics documentation. Treat the structure below as a drafting tool, not a substitute for the application guidance or a host supervisor's expectations.

How to structure the evidence

Begin with a working title and a focused research question. Explain the context with properly attributed literature rather than broad claims that nobody has studied the topic. State the contribution you expect to make and the assumptions that you need to test.

An example to adapt truthfully

The methods section should identify data, sampling or recruitment, analysis, permissions, limitations and feasible access to facilities. Example: for a study of university food waste, define which sites and weeks are observed, the measurement protocol and what will be done when data are missing. Do not promise data or institutional approval you do not possess.

Before you submit

Finish with a realistic timetable, milestones and a short statement of why the proposed host is appropriate. Compare your draft against the award's assessment criteria and revise it with legitimate academic feedback. Some programmes require a proposal at admission; others ask for it only after supervisor agreement.

  1. Reopen the actual provider's current application requirements.
  2. Check nationality, academic stage and work permission where applicable.
  3. Use only verifiable examples and follow the document format.
