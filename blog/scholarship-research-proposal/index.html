Scholarship Research Proposal Template: Questions and Methods

Build a feasible research plan

What the application asks you to show

A research proposal is a plan for investigating a specific problem. Different scholarship schemes request different lengths, headings and ethics documentation. Treat the structure below as a drafting tool, not a substitute for the application guidance or a host supervisor's expectations.

How to structure the evidence

Begin with a working title and a focused research question. Explain the context with properly attributed literature rather than broad claims that nobody has studied the topic. State the contribution you expect to make and the assumptions that you need to test.

An example to adapt truthfully

The methods section should identify data, sampling or recruitment, analysis, permissions, limitations and feasible access to facilities. Example: for a study of university food waste, define which sites and weeks are observed, the measurement protocol and what will be done when data are missing. Do not promise data or institutional approval you do not possess.

Before you submit

Finish with a realistic timetable, milestones and a short statement of why the proposed host is appropriate. Compare your draft against the award's assessment criteria and revise it with legitimate academic feedback. Some programmes require a proposal at admission; others ask for it only after supervisor agreement.

  1. Reopen the actual provider's current application requirements.
  2. Check nationality, academic stage and work permission where applicable.
  3. Use only verifiable examples and follow the document format.

A structure that most funders recognise

PartWhat it doesRough share of the length
Title and questionStates one clear research questionA few lines
BackgroundShows what is already known and the gap you will addressAbout a quarter
Aims and objectivesBreaks the question into steps you can completeShort
MethodsExplains data, methods and why they fit the questionAbout a third
TimelineShows the work fits the funded periodA short table
SignificanceSays who will use the results and why it mattersA paragraph
ReferencesKey sources you actually citeAs required

Always follow the word or page limit and any headings the funder sets; this structure is a starting point, not a replacement for their form.

Common weaknesses and how to fix them

  1. A topic instead of a question. "Water pollution in my region" is a topic. "Which sources explain most of the nitrate in the river's upper basin?" is a question you can answer.
  2. Methods that are too vague. Name the data, how you will get access to it and how you will analyse it.
  3. Too much for the time. A one-year master's project needs a narrow scope. A PhD proposal can be broader but still needs milestones.
  4. No link to the host. Explain why the department, supervisor or facilities fit the work. Do not claim a supervisor has agreed unless they have.
  5. Ignoring ethics or access. If you need people's data, fieldwork permits or lab time, say how you will arrange it.

Example: A hypothetical applicant proposing research on mobile-money use among market traders narrows it to one city, two trader groups and a six-month survey. The timeline shows fieldwork, analysis and writing, and the significance section names the local regulator as a likely user of the findings.
