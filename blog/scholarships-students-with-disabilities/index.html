Scholarships for Students With Disabilities: Accessibility and Eligibility Questions

Check the award's eligibility wording, accessibility procedures and reasonable adjustment process.

OppMatcher editorial · Evidence-first applicationsPublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Check the award's eligibility wording, accessibility procedures and reasonable adjustment process. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Check the scholarship's actual purpose

Some awards explicitly support applicants with disabilities; others are open to broader groups but have accessibility procedures. An inclusion statement does not itself create a disability-specific scholarship. Read the named programme and its current conditions.

Separate eligibility from sensitive disclosure

You should not send medical records or disability information to an unofficial aggregator. Providers may explain what evidence, if any, is required and how they handle it. Ask the scholarship office through a verified channel about accessible forms and reasonable adjustments.

Plan academic and living support

Consider accessible accommodation, assistive technology, support services and additional transport costs. Check which expenses an award covers and what help the university can provide separately. A stipend may not automatically cover specialist equipment.

Request adjustments in time

If interviews, tests or application portals create access barriers, contact the authorised programme team before the deadline. Describe the adjustment needed without providing unnecessary sensitive detail. Save the response and any updated procedure in your application tracker.

Practical example

Example: an applicant needs an accessible remote interview rather than a particular travel arrangement. They should contact the scholarship office with the specific access request and confirm how the provider handles the process.

What to check

  1. Find the specific award eligibility text
  2. Use official channels for adjustments
  3. Compare funding with accessibility expenses

Common question

Do all scholarships use the same disability eligibility and adjustment rules?

No. Programmes and institutions may differ in eligibility evidence and reasonable adjustment arrangements. Check the current provider guidance and ask its admissions or accessibility team about the requirements that apply to you.

Two separate questions: eligibility and support

Disabled applicants applying for international scholarships face two distinct questions:

  1. Can I apply, and on what terms? A few schemes adjust eligibility or offer specific routes.
  2. What support will I get if selected? Several major schemes fund disability-related costs on top of the standard award.

The UK's Disabled Students' Allowance (DSA), often mentioned in UK guidance, is mainly for UK students. International students are usually pointed to their sponsor or scholarship instead. Leeds University, for example, tells international students to talk to their sponsor about extra funding.

The university's duty is separate

In the UK, universities must make reasonable adjustments so disabled students are not substantially disadvantaged, such as extra exam time, accessible materials or assistive equipment. That duty applies whatever your scholarship. Chevening and the CSC both advise contacting the university's disability or student support service as early as possible. Scholarship funding usually covers costs beyond what the university provides. It doesn't replace the university's own obligations.

When and how to disclose

Disclosure is your choice. Schemes that offer support can only arrange it if they know. The CSC encourages scholars to disclose as early as possible so adjustments can be made, including for its events. Practical points:

  • Disclosing on an application form is usually separate from selection. Check the scheme's statement on how the information is used.
  • Ask for adjustments to the application process itself if you need them, such as extended interview time or accessible formats. UKRI, for instance, invites applicants to contact it about adjustments and says no formal diagnosis is needed to ask.
  • Keep medical or assessment evidence ready. Support assessments often need it.

Worked example: planning support before arrival

A Chevening scholar who uses a screen reader accepts their award in June. They complete Chevening's disability survey and email its welfare team, and contact the university's disability service. The university arranges accessible course materials and exam adjustments. Chevening's additional funding covers equipment the university doesn't provide. Starting in June rather than September means the support is in place for the first week of teaching.

Checklist

  1. Check whether the scheme offers a specific route or adjusted eligibility, such as the CSC's contextualised nomination.
  2. Read the scheme's disability support statement before you apply.
  3. Ask for any adjustments you need to the application process.
  4. Once selected, contact both the scheme and the university's disability service immediately.
  5. Keep evidence and assessments ready.
  6. Ask the university about accessible accommodation early. Allocation often closes months before the course starts.
  7. If equipment is funded, ask who owns it and whether you can take it home after the course.
