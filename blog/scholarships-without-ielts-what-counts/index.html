Scholarships Without IELTS: What Actually Counts as Proof of English

A scholarship that accepts alternatives to IELTS is not automatically a scholarship with no English requirement.

OppMatcher editorial · Research and application guide23 October 2026

A scholarship that accepts alternatives to IELTS is not automatically a scholarship with no English requirement. The goal is to check the exact rules of the programme you intend to use, not to treat any general guide as a confirmed open application.

Separate scholarship rules from university admission

A funding provider may allow a different English-language document while its host university still requires an approved test. Begin with the specific course admission page, then read the funding call. Make two columns for each requirement and avoid accepting a social-media statement saying that IELTS is waived for everyone. Language expectations can also change by teaching language, degree level and campus.

Find the exact type of evidence accepted

The alternatives might include an authorised English-taught-degree certificate, another recognised test, or a university assessment. A medium-of-instruction letter is useful only if the responsible institution explicitly accepts it. Check whether the provider requires a particular date, minimum grade, duration of English study or a test score issued within a specified validity period.

Ask a question that can be answered

Instead of asking a scholarship office whether IELTS is mandatory, give the exact programme name and the evidence you hold. Ask whether it satisfies the funding requirement and the university admission requirement separately. Request a link to the current rule or a written confirmation from an official institutional email address; an informal agent's message is not an admission decision.

Protect your application timetable

If a substitute document is not explicitly accepted, plan enough time to take an approved language test. Keep registration availability, result release, institution acceptance and scholarship closing dates on a single calendar. A scholarship advertised as 'without IELTS' can still be a poor fit if the programme requires another test you cannot complete in time.

Worked example

Example: a candidate completed a bachelor's degree taught in English but has no IELTS score. They should locate their chosen master's degree language policy, confirm whether a university-issued medium-of-instruction letter is accepted, and then check the scholarship's separate rule before relying on the exemption.

Action checklist

  1. Choose one named course and scholarship
  2. Record the exact alternative document and acceptance evidence
  3. Keep a fallback test booking decision date

Frequently asked question

Does 'without IELTS' mean no English proof?

No. Many routes require another acceptable form of language evidence.

“Without IELTS” does not mean “without language evidence”

A scholarship can remove its own English-test requirement while the university still asks for an accepted test or exemption. The official Chevening FAQ confirms that Chevening removed its separate English language requirement in 2020, but candidates still need to meet their chosen university's requirements and obtain an unconditional course offer. Do not interpret this as a waiver issued by every UK university.

Claim on a scholarship pageWhat to verify
“IELTS is not required”Whether another accepted English test, proof of previous English-medium education or university exemption is required.
“English-taught course”Whether the institution still demands test results for international students.
“Medium of instruction letter accepted”Exact format, previous qualification, issuer, age of proof and whether the individual course accepts it.
“Language preparation included”Whether a preparatory course truly waives the admission threshold or merely helps meet it later.

Three distinct official language routes

Chevening: Scholarship applicants no longer satisfy a separate Chevening English-test score, but the three UK course choices can have different admission rules. Record the selected universities' official language evidence in the application plan.

DAAD: German scholarship rules vary by individual DAAD award and by the German university. An English-taught programme may accept English evidence, while a German-taught programme normally requires German competence. Search DAAD's study requirements and the exact named scholarship entry before paying for a test.

Erasmus Mundus: The European Commission says students apply to the consortium operating the specific joint master's. Each consortium publishes its own selection criteria and language evidence. “Erasmus Mundus without IELTS” does not describe every consortium.

Worked example: two universities, one scholarship

Suppose a candidate intends to apply for Chevening funding and two eligible UK courses. University A's course page expressly accepts a particular English-medium degree as evidence, while University B requires a current approved language test. Chevening's own language waiver would not cancel University B's condition. The candidate should record each university's admission evidence separately.

Do not fabricate a medium-of-instruction letter or claim a language score you have not obtained. Request official confirmation from the admissions office if the course page does not resolve your particular qualification.

Ask the right question before booking a test

  1. Open the specific current scholarship announcement.
  2. Open the exact university or programme language requirements page.
  3. Write down whether tests, alternatives or case-by-case exemptions are accepted.
  4. Check accepted test names, minimum scores, validity period and deadline for evidence.
  5. Ask admissions for a written answer about a particular exemption if the rules are ambiguous.

Scholarships without IELTS 2027: check the language evidence

A scholarship may not require IELTS while its host university still requires acceptable English-language proof. A medium-of-instruction certificate, TOEFL score, previous English-taught degree or institutional exemption is valid only when the specific admissions office says it accepts that evidence.

Use the official masters scholarship route to find a real eligible course, then write to the academic department or scholarship organiser asking whether your particular certificate is acceptable. Never turn 'no IELTS requirement' into 'no English evidence required'.
