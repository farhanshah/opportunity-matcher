UK Internships for International Graduates: How to Assess Eligibility

Check contract restrictions, work permission and graduate recruitment deadlines for UK-based roles.

OppMatcher editorial · Application guidance23 October 2026

Check contract restrictions, work permission and graduate recruitment deadlines for UK-based roles. Use this guide to evaluate a specific posting against its official evidence; it is not a guarantee that any particular internship or selection process is currently open.

Check the employer's candidate requirements

Some UK programmes are designed for students undertaking a sandwich placement, some for new graduates and others for any applicant with the required skills. Read the qualification stage and course-enrolment rules instead of assuming all internships accept graduates.

Interpret sponsorship questions carefully

Employers may ask whether you currently have work authorisation and whether you might require sponsorship later. Those are separate facts. Answer according to your immigration position, role duration and the wording of the question; do not infer eligibility from a generic university careers article.

Compare working terms and costs

Ask about hourly pay, location, expected days on site, probation and whether the position is genuinely a fixed-term development role. If you would relocate within the UK, include rent and commuting costs in the decision, particularly where a role is short.

Build applications around local evidence

Describe teamwork, academic projects, customer service or technical outputs that match the vacancy. Check whether the company accepts overseas qualifications and whether it has a formal deadline or rolling recruitment. Keep applications truthful and save confirmations.

Worked example

Example: a graduate in Middlesbrough considers a six-month placement in London. They should compare net pay after housing and travel with the role's learning value and verify that their own work permission covers the proposed contract.

UK Graduate visa permission and its 2027 change

As of November 2026, the UK Graduate visa guidance permits most jobs, including internships, and self-employment, apart from professional sportsperson work. A successful applicant's permission currently lasts two years if they apply on or before 31 December 2026, or 18 months for most new applicants applying from 1 January 2027. Doctoral graduates have a three-year duration. These rules concern immigration permission, not an employer's eligibility criteria.

The official Appendix Graduate confirms that the route is unsponsored and is not itself a route to settlement. It is important to distinguish working under existing Graduate permission from requiring an employer to sponsor a later Skilled Worker application.

Worked example: a 24-month scheme

A graduate whose Graduate visa expires eight months after a proposed internship starts may be allowed to work during those eight months, but this does not prove they can complete a programme lasting 24 months. The employer might require continued permission for the entire scheme or a later visa transition. Applicants should give honest current work authorisation details and check the employer's own requirements instead of marking every sponsorship question “No” automatically.

Someone applying for a Graduate visa in December 2026 and another applying in January 2027 can receive different ordinary permission durations under the published rules. Check the application and decision dates carefully; no third-party page can grant an extension.

Payment, classification and holiday rules

Not every placement labelled an “internship” is exempt from minimum wage. The UK government's intern employment rights guidance explains that pay depends on actual worker status and whether an exemption applies, including certain placements forming part of a UK-based further or higher education course. A graduate internship in an ordinary paid worker role should not automatically be treated as unpaid merely because its title says “intern”.

Ask the employer about the contract, hours, minimum-wage treatment, supervision, holiday and whether the placement is a genuine assessed university course requirement.

Four checks before clicking Apply

  1. Read the official employer vacancy and record the exact start date and expected duration.
  2. Use your current immigration permission and its expiry date, not future assumptions, when answering work-rights questions.
  3. Check whether the employer requires sponsorship now or might require it before the programme ends.
  4. Confirm the promised pay and internship contract status against the applicable UK employment rules.
