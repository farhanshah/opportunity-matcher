Undergraduate Scholarships Abroad: A Practical Eligibility Checklist

Check entry level, secondary-school equivalence and funding before planning overseas study.

OppMatcher editorial · Research and application guidePublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Check entry level, secondary-school equivalence and funding before planning overseas study. The goal is to check the exact rules of the programme you intend to use, not to treat any general guide as a confirmed open application.

Compare school credentials before scholarship forms

Undergraduate admission depends on whether a university recognises your secondary qualification and subjects. Check age and year-of-completion conditions where an award publishes them. Do not assume an overseas institution treats every country's high-school credential as equivalent to its own final examination.

Check whether the scholarship admits freshmen

Some awards are open only to new first-year students, others to continuing students, and some to a specific university. Search for the entry year and enrolment stage. A scholarship for graduate students is not relevant to a school-leaver even when its subject and country sound appropriate.

Budget for preparatory study and relocation

Some overseas undergraduate routes include preparatory language or foundation periods. Confirm whether this time is funded and how it affects the degree start date. Consider visa fees, medical checks, housing deposits and the cost of travelling home during breaks, instead of relying on an award's headline amount.

Protect document lead times

Collect official school transcripts and certificates, and find out whether translation, authentication or a school counsellor recommendation is required. If a scholarship has an embassy route, your country's diplomatic mission may set earlier deadlines. Drafting the essay first is less useful than resolving missing credentials and application permissions.

Worked example

Example: a student with A Levels considering a Japanese government undergraduate route must check the MEXT category and embassy procedure. They should not reuse a research-student form or assume a university-only application is the correct route.

Action checklist

  1. Confirm your secondary qualification is accepted
  2. Verify first-year and age eligibility
  3. Check foundation, language and living-cost coverage

Frequently asked question

Can I apply before my final school results?

Only where the specific programme provides a conditional or predicted-results route.

Undergraduate scholarships work differently from postgraduate ones

Most widely advertised "fully funded" scholarships are for Master's study. Full undergraduate scholarships for international students exist, but they tend to have rules that postgraduate applicants never meet: strict age cut-offs, application through an embassy rather than a university, and sometimes nomination by your school. Government schemes in Japan and Korea are among the largest, and illustrate these rules clearly.

Both programmes usually include a year of language study before the degree. Embassy deadlines differ by country, so check the page of the Japanese or Korean embassy in your own country, not another country's.

University awards: nomination and early deadlines

Some of the best-known university undergraduate awards for international students sit inside the admissions process, and some need your school's involvement. The University of Toronto's Lester B. Pearson International Scholarship starts with a nomination from your high school, which happens between July and October. Only nominated students receive a private link to the scholarship application. The University of Oxford's Reach Oxford Scholarship is for students from low-income countries who, for political or financial reasons, or because suitable facilities don't exist, cannot study for a degree at home. It considers only applicants who already hold an offer of a place, and it covers all subjects except medicine. Treat any nomination or offer requirement as the first deadline in your plan.

Worked example: a timeline for a final-year school student

Imagine a student in their final school year who wants to start a degree abroad in autumn 2028:

  • March–June 2027: list target schemes and record each age cut-off against your date of birth. Check whether any require school nomination (such as Pearson, nominated between July and October), and tell your school counsellor.
  • From April 2027: watch for the Japanese embassy's MEXT undergraduate call for April 2028 entry, which may close in May or June, and the GKS guidelines.
  • September 2027–January 2028: university admission and scholarship deadlines for autumn 2028 entry in the UK, US and Canada. Many awards share the admission deadline, or come earlier.
  • Throughout: book any required English or other language tests early enough for results to arrive before the deadlines.

Exact dates change every year. Use this as an order of tasks, not a calendar.

Undergraduate scholarship checklist

  1. Check the age cut-off as an exact date of birth, not "under 25".
  2. Confirm you will have the required years of schooling by the stated date.
  3. Find out whether you apply through an embassy, a university or your school.
  4. Check your own and your parents' citizenship against the rules.
  5. Check whether a language year is part of the award, and whether it is compulsory.
  6. Record every deadline in your own time zone.
