UNDP Internships: Current Roles, Deadlines and Fit

Learn why the exact UNDP requisition matters more than a generic internship programme page.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official sources

Learn why the exact UNDP requisition matters more than a generic internship programme page. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Find the exact role

UNDP recruits for different offices, locations and specialisms. Open the individual posting in the official recruitment system and save its reference, closing time and team name. General programme pages cannot establish whether a particular position is still accepting applications.

Check practical restrictions

Study level, graduation timing, nationality, language and work-authorisation rules can vary across assignments. A remote role may still have residency or time-zone conditions. Compare what the particular job actually says with your documents before applying.

Make relevant evidence easy to find

For a project role, describe timelines, budgets or stakeholder work; for research, describe methods and findings; for communications, describe content and results. Separate work you carried out from a team's shared outcome. Keep the CV and application form consistent.

Submit and track the application

UNDP vacancies can change or close, so check the portal at submission. Record the exact posting and confirmation number, prepare supporting material requested by the role and watch official messages for updates. Do not pay a third party for access to a free official vacancy listing.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.

UNDP internship eligibility has three common academic routes

UNDP's official career FAQ describes internships for people in their final year of bachelor's study, those completing master's-level studies and recent graduates. Applicants who have graduated must start an internship within one year after graduation. Previous work experience is not required by the general internship programme, but individual vacancies may specify skills, location rules and other conditions.

Applicant's positionCheck before applying
Final-year bachelor's studentConfirm your academic year and whether the particular vacancy accepts your field and degree level
Master's-level studentConfirm current enrolment and any field-specific coursework or technical skills
Recent graduateConfirm degree completion date and that the proposed internship start falls within the provider's one-year rule

These are general UNDP eligibility rules. The exact requisition is still the final authority on location, citizenship, languages and any additional criteria. Do not conclude that every applicant from every country can apply to every vacancy.

Duration, stipend and work arrangement

UNDP says internships usually last between three and six months, with exceptions for qualifying academic-credit placements. Internships may be in-person or remote and full-time or part-time, depending on the posting. UNDP's FAQ describes a monthly stipend for eligible interns unless the intern is sponsored by another institution or government. The value depends on duty station and arrangement; this page does not invent a stipend amount for a named vacancy.

Before accepting an offer, compare the expected hours, duty station, possible relocation costs and the terms stated in the actual assignment. A remote internship can still require particular working hours or local residency.

Language and job-specific evidence

The UNDP FAQ says internship applicants need English and, where relevant, the working language of the particular office. A communications role might ask for writing samples or communications software, while a research internship might focus on data collection or analysis. Rather than using one CV for every post, copy the actual responsibilities from the official requisition into a preparation checklist and match each one with genuine experience or a work sample.
