UNESCO Internships: Application Requirements and Costs

UNESCO internship applications may place candidates in a pool; submitting does not guarantee a role.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026

UNESCO internship applications may place candidates in a pool; submitting does not guarantee a role. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Know what the internship application means

UNESCO's official internship guidance explains that candidates can be considered for assignments according to organisational needs. Being eligible or completing the form does not guarantee an offer. Review the candidate pool rules and the actual opportunity description before planning a start date.

Check the academic and language criteria

Review accepted student and recent-graduate categories, required language ability and programme-specific qualifications. Official documents may need translation. Someone studying a related subject is not necessarily eligible for every UNESCO unit or duty location.

Write a focused motivation letter

Explain your skills using examples such as research, writing, data work, communications or project coordination. Say what you contributed and how it relates to UNESCO's work. A short, accurate description of one university project is more useful than calling yourself passionate without evidence.

Budget for an unpaid internship

UNESCO's programme guidance says interns are not remunerated. Before applying to a location away from home, check travel, accommodation, visa and living costs. Keep documentation and contacts ready for the selection process, but do not make financial commitments before receiving an official placement offer.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.
