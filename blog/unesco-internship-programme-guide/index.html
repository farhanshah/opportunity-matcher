UNESCO Internships: Application Requirements and Costs

UNESCO internship applications may place candidates in a pool; submitting does not guarantee a role.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official programme evidence

UNESCO internship applications may place candidates in a pool; submitting does not guarantee a role. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Know what the internship application means

UNESCO's official internship guidance explains that candidates can be considered for assignments according to organisational needs. Being eligible or completing the form does not guarantee an offer. Review the candidate pool rules and the actual opportunity description before planning a start date.

Check the academic and language criteria

Review accepted student and recent-graduate categories, required language ability and programme-specific qualifications. Official documents may need translation. Someone studying a related subject is not necessarily eligible for every UNESCO unit or duty location.

Write a focused motivation letter

Explain your skills using examples such as research, writing, data work, communications or project coordination. Say what you contributed and how it relates to UNESCO's work. A short, accurate description of one university project is more useful than calling yourself passionate without evidence.

Budget for an unpaid internship

UNESCO's programme guidance says interns are not remunerated. Before applying to a location away from home, check travel, accommodation, visa and living costs. Keep documentation and contacts ready for the selection process, but do not make financial commitments before receiving an official placement offer.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.

UNESCO eligibility: age, qualifications and languages

UNESCO's official Internship Programme has more specific general academic and language rules than many internship directories mention. Applicants must be at least 20 years old. Its accepted university study or graduation routes include the following:

Applicant's study routeUNESCO's published condition
Bachelor's degree in progressCurrently in the third or final year of university study.
Advanced degree in progressCurrently enrolled in a master's, PhD or comparable second university degree.
Recent university graduateCompleted a bachelor's, master's or PhD within the previous 12 months, using the diploma completion date.
Technical or vocational trainingIn the final year of specialised studies or recently graduated within the 12-month window.

UNESCO expects strong written and spoken English or French, with another language sometimes relevant to a particular assignment. Applicants also need suitable office and computer skills, communication skills and the ability to work as part of a team. Always confirm the chosen notice, especially when it concerns a specialised office.

The UNESCO talent pool is not a confirmed internship offer

UNESCO's application system accepts candidates into an internship talent pool. Hiring managers may select interns from that pool when an assignment becomes available. A submitted application can remain in the pool for six months, and an eligible applicant might never receive an offer. UNESCO says applicants who receive no update after that period should treat the application as unsuccessful.

This is different from assuming that a general UNESCO programme page is an open position with an agreed location and start date. An internship notice may describe a particular candidate pool rather than a vacancy reserved for the next applicant. For example, UNESCO's All Sectors/Bureaus internship notice describes a pool for assignments across offices. Its displayed 31 December 2026 application date belongs to that specific notice, not to every internship or to a guaranteed placement.

Budget for an internship that UNESCO does not pay for

UNESCO states that interns are not remunerated. The usual duration is one to six months. Applicants are responsible for arranging and paying for their travel and necessary visas. They must also provide proof of suitable health insurance for the whole internship in the country concerned. UNESCO's limited workplace accident coverage does not replace comprehensive health insurance.

A practical budget should distinguish one-time costs from monthly expenses. Use this worksheet before accepting any offer:

Cost to investigateExample record to obtain
Travel and visaOfficial visa fees, realistic travel itinerary and entry rules for the duty station.
AccommodationMonthly rent, security deposit and transport costs near the assigned office.
Health insuranceCoverage for the actual destination and complete internship dates.
Living costsFood, local travel, phone and emergency buffer for one to six months.
Income or external fundingAny university grant or other eligible source that is independently confirmed.

UNESCO does not offer an automatic stipend just because another UN agency might. Always check whether an unpaid placement is practical before making relocation commitments.

What to prepare before starting the application

UNESCO advises preparing a CV or résumé and motivation letter before beginning. It says the application session provides roughly one hour to finish, and submissions must be made in English or French. Applicants whose original documents are in another language may need unofficial translations in PDF format. Keep contact details current for the six-month pool period.

  1. Choose an official UNESCO internship notice matching your education and interests.
  2. Check its current deadline and whether it describes a talent pool rather than a specific confirmed assignment.
  3. Prepare an accurate CV, motivation letter and education records before opening the application form.
  4. Verify the duty station, insurance, visa and financing arrangements before making commitments.
  5. Save the submission confirmation, then review your application status without assuming placement.
