UNHCR Internships: Application and Eligibility

Check UNHCR's recruitment system and the individual duty-station requirements.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official programme evidence

Check UNHCR's recruitment system and the individual duty-station requirements. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Where the vacancies appear

UNHCR directs applicants to its official recruitment platform and notes that local or regional offices can advertise opportunities as well. A programme information page is not itself a live vacancy. Search by location, job category and closing date.

Read legal and academic conditions

A placement can have requirements linked to degree status, graduation timing, language or residence. Confirm whether you need work authorisation for the relevant country. Do not interpret a global recruitment page as a promise that every post is open to all nationalities.

Write about relevant experience

Examples from volunteer programmes, community engagement, research, data operations or communication may be relevant, depending on the post. Describe what you personally achieved. Match the listed tasks instead of inserting general claims about humanitarian work into every application.

Protect your personal details

UNHCR warns that it does not charge recruitment fees. Use official application channels and avoid responding to requests for payments or documents sent through an unknown messenger. Keep the requisition link and official confirmation to identify legitimate correspondence.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.

UNHCR eligibility requirements from the current official programme

The UNHCR internship information page provides more specific academic and language rules than a generic international-internship list. The programme accepts current university students and recent graduates, subject to actual vacancies and local requirements.

A recent graduate who finished university 20 months ago may meet the graduation window, but needs to confirm the posting's actual field, skill and language needs. A person who graduated more than two years ago should not assume the general recent-graduate route still applies.

Duration, financial support and insurance are separate decisions

UNHCR internships normally last two to six months, with extensions possible up to a cumulative eight months for full-time internships or twelve months for part-time internships. Placement location depends on the receiving office; headquarters, service centres and field offices have different requirements. Remote or hybrid work needs managerial approval.

UNHCR states that interns without financial support from another party receive a contribution toward food, local transport and living expenses. It may reimburse return travel for interns recruited from outside the duty-station country, unless a sponsor provides that support. Interns are still responsible for securing visas and covering the visa costs, although UNHCR may help with relevant documentation.

Medical insurance is the intern's responsibility. UNHCR requires proof of coverage valid for the entire placement and appropriate to the actual destination. UNHCR does not automatically cover medical insurance or emergency medical evacuation costs. Read its programme guidance before budgeting.

Worked example: moving for a Geneva placement

Suppose a university student is shortlisted for an internship at UNHCR headquarters in Geneva. The student should separately check: whether a food and local-transport allowance will be paid, which travel costs can be reimbursed, the cost of obtaining a suitable Swiss visa, and the actual health insurance required. An advertised allowance is not a promise of fully funded accommodation, so the student should not book non-refundable travel before confirming the terms with the official office.

Applying safely through the official recruitment platform

  1. Check UNHCR's official application instructions and find a named internship vacancy.
  2. Verify the role reference, closing date, location and exact study or recent-graduate requirements.
  3. Prepare an accurate profile and examples of relevant experience, such as data, logistics, law, community engagement or administrative work, according to the duties.
  4. Submit through UNHCR's authorised recruitment platform and keep your confirmation.
  5. Never pay recruitment, interview or training fees. UNHCR states recruitment is free.

If you are comparing different United Nations agencies, read the UNDP and UNICEF internship guides. The eligibility windows and allowances are not interchangeable.
