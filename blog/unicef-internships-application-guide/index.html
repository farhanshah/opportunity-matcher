UNICEF Internships: Find Open Roles and Apply

Read each UNICEF posting for location, nationality and academic requirements before applying.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official programme evidence

Read each UNICEF posting for location, nationality and academic requirements before applying. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Use the vacancy record, not the overview

The UNICEF internship overview is helpful for learning about the programme, but individual vacancies carry the real closing dates and candidate requirements. Find the current posting and record its reference number. A summary from months ago is not evidence that the vacancy remains open.

Check nationality restrictions carefully

Some UNICEF internships are intended for candidates from a specific country or with the right to work at a duty station. Review the exact role's nationality, education and language requirements. Do not assume 'international' means every nationality can apply for every post.

Tailor documents to the work

A research role may value methodology, data tools or report writing, while a communications assignment might ask for audience analysis or content production. Use real examples and avoid adding skills you cannot demonstrate. Confirm whether the portal asks for a cover letter, transcript or other proof.

Consider the practical terms

Pay, remote arrangements, insurance, the contract period and any visa implications can vary. Check the actual UNICEF notice for this information, along with the latest application deadline. Submit only through the authorised provider channel and keep the receipt.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.

UNICEF's general internship eligibility rules

UNICEF publishes its programme rules for students and recent graduates on its official internships page. These are screening conditions, not a guarantee that any particular office will accept your application. Always compare the individual vacancy's requirements with the programme overview.

A candidate who is 22 and graduated 18 months ago might meet the age and recent-graduate windows, but that does not establish eligibility for a posting limited to residents or nationals of one country. The same person may qualify for one UNICEF internship and be excluded from another.

How long an internship lasts and what UNICEF pays

UNICEF says internships normally run six to 26 weeks, on a full-time or part-time basis. Placements can be on-site or remote depending on the vacancy and the supervising office. The published programme guidance describes a monthly stipend for interns. Some eligible assignments may also receive a one-time contribution toward travel and visa costs when funds are available. Do not treat possible travel help as guaranteed or assume a universal stipend amount.

Before accepting an offer, list the location, work schedule, expected living costs, insurance, visa or residence requirements and any financial support stated in the specific notice. Check whether an advertised remote arrangement restricts where the applicant may be physically located.

Read UNICEF's internship FAQs for stipend, duration, remote-work and selection questions.

Case study: a vacancy restricted to Sri Lankan nationals

An actual UNICEF advertisement was titled Internship - Social & Behavior Change, 26 weeks, Colombo, Sri Lanka (Sri Lankan nationals only). The role was associated with posting number 595893 and a closing date of 22 October 2026. This listing is now historical, not a current invitation to apply.

The title alone establishes an important nationality restriction that a generic “UNICEF internships worldwide” article would miss. The job notice also refers to a relevant social and behavioural sciences background. Applying through the correct UNICEF vacancy

  1. Open UNICEF's official internship vacancy list and choose a posting with a current deadline.
  2. Read the job's title and eligibility restrictions, including nationality, education, office language and duty station.
  3. Match each required task to an example from your real coursework, projects, volunteering or previous work.
  4. Complete the application through UNICEF's official system; retain the role reference and confirmation.
  5. Do not assume that applying guarantees an interview or employment after the internship.
