US Graduate Assistantship vs Fellowship: Tuition and Work Obligations

Compare assistantships that require work with fellowships and scholarships that have different terms.

OppMatcher editorial · Evidence-first applicationsPublished 23 October 2026Updated 23 November 2026 with official sources

Compare assistantships that require work with fellowships and scholarships that have different terms. This is an evergreen decision guide. Always confirm the latest application status, scheme conditions and closing time on the official page.

Understand assistantship duties

Teaching and research assistantships are often employment-linked awards involving work for a university department. Duties, hours and eligibility can vary. Read the graduate school's funding agreement and the department's appointment conditions before comparing it with an award that has no assigned work.

Check tuition and stipend separately

Some packages include tuition remission, a stipend and health benefits; others include only one component. Record whether university fees, summer months and insurance are included. Funding letters can be more precise than general statements on a university website.

Compare academic consequences

A teaching assistantship may develop classroom skills but consume time during coursework. A research assistantship can connect with a thesis project, but the assigned work may not be identical to your own research goals. Ask the department about workload and expectations.

Review international student restrictions

Employment and work-hour conditions can interact with immigration status. Check the university's international-student office and current US government guidance for your situation. Do not assume that funding or admission alone establishes permission for off-campus work.

Practical example

Example: two doctoral offers list similar annual support, but one pays 12 months with a research duty and the other pays only during the academic year. Compare net tuition, summer support and expected work before choosing.

What to check

  1. Record tuition remission and fees
  2. Check required work hours
  3. Ask about funding over summer

Common question

Is a US graduate assistantship the same as a fellowship?

Not necessarily. Assistantships often involve teaching or research responsibilities, while fellowships can have different award conditions. Compare work duties, tuition remission, stipend, duration and renewal rules.

What each one is

EducationUSA, the US State Department's advising network, lists the main types of US university support for graduate students as fellowships, teaching assistantships, research assistantships and administrative assistantships. It notes that working as a teaching or research assistant in exchange for a tuition waiver and/or stipend is very common at PhD level.

  • A fellowship is funding without a work requirement. University graduate schools describe merit fellowships as a stipend and tuition support with no job duties attached.
  • An assistantship is a job. You teach (TA), work on a funded research project (RA) or work in an office (GA) in exchange for a stipend and, usually, a tuition waiver. The Fulbright Commission's EducationUSA advice for UK students puts the typical commitment at 15 to 20 hours a week.

The F-1 visa rule that shapes assistantships

US Immigration and Customs Enforcement's SEVIS guidance lets F-1 students work on campus up to 20 hours a week while school is in session, and full-time during official vacations. University international offices add the practical details:

  • The 20 hours is a total across all on-campus jobs, and it includes work done under a scholarship, fellowship or assistantship. George Washington University notes that a TA working 15 hours can only work 5 more elsewhere.
  • Hours can't be averaged across weeks.
  • Some universities treat a standard half-time assistantship as the full 20 hours, so you can't take a second campus job.
  • Working over the limit is a violation of status.

How to read a US funding offer

A US funding letter can look generous and still leave a gap. Ask:

  1. Is the tuition waiver full or partial, and does it cover mandatory fees, such as student activity or technology fees?
  2. Is health insurance included or subsidised?
  3. Is the stipend paid over nine months or twelve? Is summer funding guaranteed?
  4. How many hours a week is the assistantship, and what are the duties?
  5. For how many years is funding guaranteed, and on what conditions?
  6. Is the stipend taxable? Most US stipends are, and tax treaties vary by country.

Worked example: two PhD offers

Offer A: a five-year package with a one-year fellowship, then four years as a TA at 20 hours a week, a full tuition waiver and health insurance included.
Offer B: an RA position at 20 hours a week on a professor's grant, with a full waiver, insurance not included and funding guaranteed "subject to grant renewal".

Offer A gives a fellowship year to settle in and do coursework without teaching, and it guarantees five years. Offer B's RA work may overlap with your thesis research, which can be an advantage, but its funding depends on the grant. Both use your full F-1 on-campus allowance during term. If B's insurance costs a few thousand dollars a year, that comes out of the stipend. The better offer depends on the amounts, but the questions above reveal the trade-offs that headline stipends hide.

Checklist

  1. Confirm whether each award has work duties.
  2. Add up assistantship and other campus hours against the 20-hour limit.
  3. Get the tuition, fees, insurance and stipend terms in writing.
  4. Check the guarantee period and renewal conditions.
  5. Ask current international students in the department how it works in practice.

Graduate assistantship or fellowship: which should you choose?

Choose between a graduate assistantship or fellowship by comparing the written offer. Assistantships often require teaching, research or administrative hours. A fellowship may focus on study or research without the same work duties, but its renewal and reporting rules can be different.

When comparing assistantships and fellowships, ask whether tuition is waived, which fees remain, whether health insurance is included, what the stipend covers, how many work hours are expected and whether summer funding continues. International students should also check university immigration guidance before accepting work duties.
