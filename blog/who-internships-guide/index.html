WHO Internships: How to Read a Vacancy Posting

WHO internship eligibility is determined by individual postings, not old internship lists.

OppMatcher editorialPublished 23 October 2026Updated 3 November 2026 with official programme evidence

WHO internship eligibility is determined by individual postings, not old internship lists. This guide explains a programme route and does not claim that applications are currently open.

Find an exact WHO opening

Check the official WHO careers system for a vacancy's title, reference number, date and duty station. A health-related programme description is not proof of an advertised place. If a search engine caches an earlier vacancy, trust the current provider status.

Check your academic route

A specific internship may require several years of university study or allow recent graduates for a defined period. Confirm the field of study, age or enrolment conditions against that exact post. Education and professional licensing rules are not identical across WHO assignments.

Consider placement logistics

Look for the official work arrangement, internship duration, start date, language requirements and any funding or insurance details. A placement in a regional office can have different practical requirements from one at headquarters. Do not assume a remote label removes legal residency conditions.

Write task-specific evidence

Relevant experience could include nutrition assessment, public health research, statistics, administrative systems or communications. Choose an example that shows your actual contribution, then explain the outcome accurately. Your application should answer the duties listed rather than a generic description of WHO's mission.

Before submitting your application

Save the official vacancy or programme link, record the current application status and verify the deadline and time zone. Check nationality, academic level, language conditions and any employment or residence limits. If the information is missing, do not assume you qualify. Contact the provider rather than acting on an outdated copy of the call.

Important status check: the WHO internship programme was paused

WHO's December 2025 human-resources report, document EB158/52, page 3, says the global internship programme had been paused since the end of January 2025 because of the financial crisis. WHO's general internship information page still describes normal eligibility and application procedures. A policy description does not, by itself, confirm that recruitment has resumed.

As of this guide's review on 3 November 2026, we have not independently verified a new programme-wide reopening. Do not describe this as an open internship just because the historical WHO programme page or an old vacancy appears in search results. Only treat a specific position as available if the current WHO careers system displays an application route and valid deadline.

The published general eligibility rules

The following rules explain WHO's internship framework. They are not proof of currently open applications. The exact advertised post, if one becomes available, can impose additional education, language, duty-station or practical conditions.

RequirementPublished WHO programme rule
AgeAt least 20 years old at the time of application.
University studiesEnrolment in a qualifying higher education programme, or applying within 18 months after completing a formal qualification.
Undergraduate studyAt least three years of full-time university study completed before the internship starts.
LanguagesFluent in at least one working language of the assignment office.
Nationality and family rulesA valid passport from a WHO Member State, no prohibited close family relationship with WHO staff.
Previous WHO internshipApplicants must not already have participated in WHO's internship programme.

Internship duration and recruitment process, when vacancies are offered

WHO's general programme guidance describes full-time placements lasting six to 24 consecutive weeks. Applications are submitted to a specific vacancy through the Stellis system, not by sending an unsolicited CV or application to WHO offices. WHO's internship FAQs say only successful candidates are contacted; an applicant who hears nothing four to eight weeks after the posting closes may assume they were not shortlisted.

The FAQs also explain that WHO may provide financial support for eligible interns, varying by duty station and the specific vacancy. WHO provides medical and accident insurance for the internship period, but applicants should still investigate accommodation, local living costs and visa requirements rather than assume their stay will be fully funded.

Example: why a general internship overview is not an application

Imagine a public-health student who is 22, has completed three undergraduate years and can work in English. That student may meet the general age, study and language conditions. But they still have no application to submit unless an exact WHO internship vacancy is currently advertised and accepting applications. This example illustrates why eligibility and vacancy availability are separate checks.

What to check if recruitment resumes

  1. Inspect WHO careers for a specific internship vacancy, not just the general programme link.
  2. Verify the current role's identifier, deadline, location, working arrangement and whether the official application portal is accepting submissions.
  3. Check programme-level rules against the actual vacancy conditions and your qualifications.
  4. Tailor your motivation and work examples to real job responsibilities. Apply using WHO's official system only.
  5. Recheck the programme's current status and save the source date. Use our free application readiness checklist as a planning tool, not as evidence that recruitment is open.
