Application guidance

Before applying

Short courses, youth programmes and exchanges vary in funding. Some cover fees but not flights or housing. Check institution or organiser credentials, course credit, age rules, visa requirements and total costs. Check the actual sponsor, degree stage, citizenship and residence restrictions, then record each application stage separately.

  1. Check the named provider and current programme rules.
  2. Record eligibility, admission and the deadline time zone.
  3. Compare actual costs and funding components.

Use the free application checklist and browse current opportunities.

Compare exchange formats before applying

A credit-bearing university exchange, summer school, conference and volunteer programme may have completely different costs and approval routes. A certificate doesn't guarantee academic credit. Check the organiser's current conditions and whether your institution recognises the activity.

TypeFirst check
University exchangeHome and host agreement, course recognition and funding
Summer schoolCourse fee, travel costs and housing arrangements
Youth conferenceSelection criteria and registration payment policy
VolunteeringActual host, duties, insurance and legal entry

Browse short courses, conferences and volunteering without treating a generic programme page as evidence of an open intake.

Cost worksheet and organiser checks

List the course fee, flights, visa, rent, meals, transport, insurance and deposits. Mark only costs the official sponsor explicitly covers. A tuition waiver might leave the full travel budget to the applicant. If your course requires credit transfer, obtain approval from your home department before making non-refundable bookings.

  1. Find the invitation on the organiser's own website.
  2. Confirm eligibility, dates and refund conditions for this intake.
  3. Check university recognition and visa permission separately.
  4. Compare the total self-funded amount, not a “fully funded” headline.
  5. Save your application confirmation and avoid sharing passwords or payment details with unverified contacts.
