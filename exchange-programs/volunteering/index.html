How to find the right programme

International volunteering includes short local projects, professional UN volunteer assignments and online task-based programmes. These are different from university exchange programmes and should not be evaluated using the same funding assumptions.

Check the legal organiser, duties, safeguarding practices and financial arrangements. Online volunteering does not necessarily provide remuneration, a visa or a pathway to international employment. Field assignments can require experience and specific duty-station conditions.

The official UN Volunteers portal distinguishes opportunities and conditions. Confirm the posted assignment, expected time commitment and candidate eligibility rather than relying on an intermediary promising guaranteed selection.

Funded volunteering routes compared

Genuine international volunteering programmes do not charge you to take part: they cover your basic costs instead. The two largest funded routes are the European Solidarity Corps and UN Volunteers.

Rules people miss

  • For the European Solidarity Corps you must be 18 to 30 at the start of the activity, and you generally cannot join a new individual volunteering project if you have already done one longer than 59 days.
  • Organisations recruit from the portal database, so a complete, specific profile matters as much as direct applications.
  • For UN Volunteers, your category (for example Youth or Specialist) decides which assignments you can apply for; see our UN Volunteers guide.

Warning signs

Be careful with "volunteer abroad" offers that charge large programme fees, promise certificates or jobs, or ask for payment before giving you a written description of the role and host. Funded schemes publish their conditions openly and never sell places.

Example: A hypothetical 24-year-old living in Georgia (a partner country) could register on the European Youth Portal and apply for a six-month Solidarity Corps project in Spain, with travel, housing and food covered. A 32-year-old would be too old for standard Solidarity Corps volunteering and should look at UN Volunteers instead.

What to check before applying

  1. Identify the latest official programme or individual vacancy announcement.
  2. Check applicant citizenship, residence, course or career stage and nomination requirements.
  3. Record exact deadlines, documents, conditions and funding from the current source.
