Is the event selective, funded or simply paid admission?

A conference invitation can describe a competitive scholarship, a discounted registration fee or ordinary admission to a paid event. Those are very different offers. Before applying, find the legal organiser, the programme agenda and the written selection terms on its official site. A social-media post describing an event as “fully funded” is not enough.

OfferEvidence to checkWhat it does not prove
Conference ticketFee schedule, venue, session access and refund policyTravel and accommodation are paid
Travel grantOfficial award conditions and reimbursement procedureEvery attendee qualifies or will receive money upfront
Competitive youth fellowshipEligibility, selection process, named organiser and expected activitySelection is guaranteed by payment of an application fee

Example: An event announces a “fully funded seat” but later asks applicants to arrange their own airfare and visa. The correct calculation separates registration, travel, lodging, meals and insurance. Check which items the organiser explicitly covers and whether the award is reimbursement after attendance.

Check organisers and invitation claims

Find the event independently from the host institution's or organiser's own website. If the event claims to be associated with a university, international organisation or public body, confirm that relationship through that body's published information. A logo on an invitation is not verification. Check whether the conference offers workshops, research presentations or a certificate, and whether the outcome has any recognised academic credit.

  1. Record the organiser, official URL, location and dates.
  2. Compare eligibility requirements, permitted countries, age range and language rules with your own details.
  3. Read selection conditions and separate any paid registration from an actual scholarship.
  4. Check cancellation and refund terms before paying; never send a payment to an unknown personal account.
  5. Confirm visa and entry rules with the destination's official authority, rather than relying on an invitation alone.
