How to find the right programme

Policy and AI fellowships can be organised as visiting research, training, governance research or professional placements. Some programmes recruit early-career candidates while others require advanced research or policy experience.

AI governance opportunities may focus on technical evaluations, regulation, public administration or social consequences of emerging technology. A generic interest in AI is usually less useful than a demonstrated writing, analysis or research project.

Climate, journalism and human-rights fellowships may have fieldwork, location and output requirements. Before applying, review the organiser's purpose, proposed work, travel policy and whether you must hold permission to work in the host country.

Choose the project, not just the fellowship title

Policy, climate and AI governance programmes may ask fellows to prepare a policy brief, analyse data, conduct interviews or produce a research report. The important questions are what output is expected, which methods you can use and how the provider judges the applicant's career stage.

These are illustrative outputs, not claims about positions currently advertised. Funding could support an employer, a host institution or the individual fellow depending on the scheme. Check the actual award terms.

Turn an interest into an actionable question

“Make AI safer” is too broad for a short project. A narrower research question states the system or policy being examined, the available evidence, how options will be compared and what cannot be concluded. A clear writing sample can show this reasoning without overstating results.

  1. Find the provider's current dated call and confirm application status.
  2. Check degree, career stage, location, residence and employment requirements.
  3. Identify the real deliverables and supervision or host arrangements.
  4. Prepare an authentic writing or technical sample relevant to those deliverables.
  5. Confirm costs covered, the application format and the closing time zone.

What to check before applying

  1. Identify the latest official programme or individual vacancy announcement.
  2. Check applicant citizenship, residence, course or career stage and nomination requirements.
  3. Record exact deadlines, documents, conditions and funding from the current source.
