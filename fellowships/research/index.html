Major postdoctoral fellowships compared

The largest postdoctoral fellowships for international researchers are portable, competitive awards that you apply for together with a host. The Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) and the Humboldt Foundation are the two most important in Europe, and their rules are very different.

ItemMSCA Postdoctoral FellowshipsHumboldt Research Fellowship
Who can applyResearchers of any nationality with a PhD and at most eight years of research experience after itResearchers from outside Germany: postdocs within four years of the PhD, experienced researchers within twelve
WhereEuropean Fellowships: 12–24 months in an EU or associated country. Global Fellowships: 24–36 months outside Europe with a return phase6–24 months at a research institution in Germany
Mobility ruleYou must not have lived or worked in the host country for more than 12 of the 36 months before the deadlineNone of the same kind, but your project must be with a host in Germany
TimingOne call a year (the 2026 call closed on 9 September 2026; the next is expected to close in September 2027)Selection meetings several times a year; calls can be capped
How you applyJointly with the host institution through the EU Funding and Tenders PortalDirectly to the Humboldt Foundation, with a host's confirmation

The rules that rule people out

For MSCA, the eight years are counted in full-time-equivalent research experience from the date the PhD was awarded, and the mobility rule counts any residence or main activity, including study and remote work. The European Commission publishes a self-assessment tool for the experience rule; use it before you invest time in a proposal. Each researcher may submit only one MSCA proposal per call, and low-scoring proposals face resubmission limits the following year.

Start with the host

Both fellowships need a host who has agreed to supervise the project and helps shape the proposal. Universities often run internal deadlines and proposal-writing support months before the official deadline, so contact hosts early in the year.

Example: A hypothetical Pakistani researcher who finished a PhD in Malaysia in 2024 and has worked there since could apply for an MSCA European Fellowship with a host in the Netherlands, because they have not lived in the Netherlands recently. They could not use a host in Malaysia for the outgoing phase of a Global Fellowship. See also our Humboldt guide.

How to check eligibility

Research fellowships often require a supervisor, professional sponsor or particular mobility history. Read the eligibility details before drafting a proposal. Check the official eligibility rules of the exact programme and office you apply to, because each organisation sets its own.

  1. Check the official source and exact programme identity.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualification and location restrictions.
  3. Verify admissions, nominations and document requirements.
  4. Record the final provider deadline and funding terms.
