Application guidance

Before applying

An internship programme overview is not proof of an open vacancy. Check the named job ID, recruitment system, location, pay, student status and deadline. Remote work may still be restricted by country. Check the actual sponsor, degree stage, citizenship and residence restrictions, then record each application stage separately.

  1. Check the named provider and current programme rules.
  2. Record eligibility, admission and the deadline time zone.
  3. Compare actual costs and funding components.

Use the free application checklist and browse current opportunities.

Choose the right internship listing

A particular role and a programme overview are different. If an official job board lists “Communications Intern” with its own requisition number, location and deadline, that is a role you can check individually. A university placement catalogue or an international organisation's internship scheme page can describe an established programme without confirming any individual opening. Some organisations also accept applications to a candidate pool without guaranteeing a placement.

SourceWhat is establishedWhat remains to check
Specific official vacancyJob title, reference ID and stated closing dateApplicant restrictions, exact local closing time and current status
Official candidate poolHow the provider receives profiles for possible future placementsWhether the pool is open and how an individual placement is selected
General internship programmeTypical purpose or application pathwayWhether any current intake or specific placement exists at all

OppMatcher separates recently verified individual openings from official internship programme research. An old deadline or a general programme page should never be enough to show an open vacancy.

Compare the real cost and eligibility

When comparing paid and unpaid placements, write down accommodation, travel, insurance, commuting, meals and relocation deposits alongside the allowance. A paid internship in an expensive city can still leave a shortfall. A remote internship can still require residence or authorisation in a particular country. Check the provider's notice rather than assuming that remote means worldwide.

Before writing a cover letter, confirm whether the position requires current student status, recent graduation, a particular degree level, language skills or work permission. Requirements differ even between vacancies from the same organisation.

Use genuine examples if you have limited experience

Course projects, volunteering, student society responsibilities and personal portfolios can show relevant skills when presented accurately. For each example, name a task, describe your own action and state a result you can support. Do not present a simulation as paid employment.

  1. Save the provider's actual job URL and ID.
  2. Record the current deadline and any nationality or residence restrictions.
  3. Match the job tasks to two relevant, verifiable examples.
  4. Prepare transcripts, references or work-permission evidence if requested.
  5. Apply through the named official portal and retain the confirmation.

For differences between provider systems, read the UNICEF and UNDP internship application guides.
