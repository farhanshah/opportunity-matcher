How to find the right programme

Internships in Japan differ substantially between laboratory programmes, university exchanges and company placements. Some are conducted in English while others require Japanese communication or local enrolment.

Start by confirming whether the programme accepts overseas applicants and whether it provides visa documents, accommodation or a travel allowance. University-linked summer schemes can have earlier nomination deadlines than general job portals.

The official laboratory or employer description controls the technical field, duration and closing date. Prepare specific project evidence that matches its selection criteria rather than treating Japan as one uniform internship market.

Vulcanus in detail

Vulcanus is the best-known funded industrial internship in Japan. Recent cycles have run from August to March, with a short seminar on Japan, an intensive Japanese language course in Tokyo and then a placement at a Japanese company. Each cycle depends on an EU grant, so the call can change or be cancelled. Hundreds of students apply each year for a small number of places, and you must be enrolled at an EU or associated-country university when you apply.

If you are not from the EU

For students from outside the EU, the most realistic routes are an exchange or internship agreement through your own university, or a scholarship that leads to research in Japan, such as MEXT. Be cautious with agencies that charge high placement fees for unpaid internships; check what the host company actually provides before paying.

Example: A hypothetical Polish mechanical engineering master's student fits Vulcanus and should apply in the autumn call. A Pakistani engineering student does not qualify for Vulcanus, so they should ask their university about partner institutions in Japan or look at the Japan scholarship routes.

What to check before applying

  1. Identify the latest official programme or individual vacancy announcement.
  2. Check applicant citizenship, residence, course or career stage and nomination requirements.
  3. Record exact deadlines, documents, conditions and funding from the current source.
