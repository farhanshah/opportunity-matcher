How the CERN Summer Student Programme works

CERN's Summer Student Programme brings undergraduate and master's students in physics, engineering, computer science and mathematics to Geneva for a supervised project, together with a lecture programme. Students of all nationalities can apply, but the length of stay and some conditions differ between nationals of CERN Member States and Non-Member States.

ItemWhat CERN's recent calls say
Length8 to 13 weeks in the European summer; recent calls limited Non-Member State students to 8 weeks
AllowanceA subsistence allowance of about 93 CHF per calendar day (net), with CERN health insurance included
TravelA travel contribution depending on individual circumstances
HousingHelp finding accommodation on or near the CERN site, not free housing
DeadlineLate January each year (for example 26 January 2026)

Where to apply

Most students apply on the CERN careers website. Nationals of Canada, Japan and the United States must apply through their own national programmes instead. The application needs a CV in English or French, an academic reference letter and transcripts. At the end of the stay, every summer student writes a short report on their project.

Making the application stronger

CERN projects are technical, so the application should show concrete skills: programming languages, data analysis, electronics or lab work, with a project or coursework example for each. Ask your referee early, because a missing reference after the late-January deadline usually means the application is not considered.

Example: A hypothetical third-year electrical engineering student from a Non-Member State should plan for an 8-week stay, ask a lecturer for a reference in December and submit before the end of January, listing the specific tools they have used.

Check the application route

Before preparing an application, confirm citizenship, residence, study level or employment stage, and the correct route for the current cycle. A government scholarship may require a country nomination or university admission; an internship needs a specific job notice and work-location details; a research fellowship may require a host.
