How Commonwealth Shared Scholarships work

Commonwealth Shared Scholarships are one of the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission's three master's programmes. They are jointly funded by the CSC and participating UK universities, and they are only for approved master's courses at those universities, not for undergraduate or PhD study.

ItemWhat the CSC guidance says
Who can applyCitizens of eligible low and middle income Commonwealth countries who could not afford UK study without the scholarship
CourseAn approved full-time taught master's course at a participating UK university; the list of eligible courses is published each year
FundingApproved tuition fees in full, return airfare at the start and end, and a monthly living allowance
2027/28 timingApplications opened on 10 November 2026 for study starting in September or October 2027, with a closing date in early December
AgeNo age limit

Two applications, and the university chooses

You apply for the scholarship through the CSC's online application system and, separately, for admission to the course through the university's own admissions process. Each participating university runs its own selection and puts forward a set number of candidates to the CSC, which then checks eligibility. You may apply for more than one course and university, but you can accept only one Shared Scholarship offer.

Common reasons for ineligibility

  • Applying for a course that is not on the approved list for that year.
  • Having already studied or worked for a year or more in a high-income country, which participating universities list as a bar.
  • Applying for a second UK master's degree without a strong justification.
  • Submitting documents outside the CSC system: the CSC does not accept anything sent another way.

Example: A hypothetical public-health graduate from an eligible country should check the 2027/28 list of approved courses in November, apply to the chosen university's course and complete the CSC application before the December deadline, then wait for the university's own shortlist.
