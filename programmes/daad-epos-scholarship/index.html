DAAD EPOS Scholarships: Development-Related Courses

Programme research and source checks, updated 11 November 2026

Who the programme is for

DAAD's Development-Related Postgraduate Courses programme supports eligible graduates from developing countries to study selected development-related postgraduate courses at German institutions. The award is linked to specific courses and candidates' development-related professional preparation.

Latest official-source information

The DAAD guidance says applications are sent directly to the relevant postgraduate course, and the university selection process then recommends candidates for a DAAD scholarship. The date is set by the individual eligible course rather than a single application deadline for every applicant.

Organiser: DAAD. Read current official instructions ↗

How EPOS applications actually work

EPOS (Development-Related Postgraduate Courses) is a DAAD scholarship for professionals from developing countries, but you do not apply to DAAD. You apply directly to one of the master's or PhD courses on the official EPOS list, and the course selects candidates for DAAD funding.

RequirementWhat DAAD's call says
NationalityA national of a country on the EPOS eligibility list (the OECD-DAC list, with some exceptions)
DegreeA relevant bachelor's degree, usually four years, with above-average results (often the upper third), normally no more than six years old
ExperienceAt least two years of relevant professional experience after the bachelor's degree, at the time of application
ResidenceNot living in Germany for more than 15 months at the application deadline
FundingMonthly payments of 992 euros for master's students and higher rates for doctoral candidates, plus insurance and travel allowances, for 12 to 42 months

Apply a year ahead, course by course

Each course on the EPOS list sets its own deadline, usually between summer and October of the year before the course starts. For example, a course starting in October 2027 typically closes in autumn 2026. You can apply to up to three courses, but you must list them in order of priority on the DAAD forms and send a full application to each university. Documents sent to DAAD itself are not forwarded.

Documents that are often rejected

Work certificates must be on letterhead, signed, dated and stamped, and must show exact employment dates covering at least two years after your first degree. Many courses also want a letter from your employer and, if possible, a guarantee of re-employment after the course. If you already hold a master's degree, you need a strong argument for why a second one should be funded.

Example: A hypothetical water engineer who graduated four years ago and has worked for three years at a utility company could apply to two water-management courses on the EPOS list before their autumn deadlines, ranking them on the DAAD forms and including a stamped work certificate.

How to prepare and avoid eligibility mistakes

Start with the DAAD course list and check the applicable programme, country of residence, first degree, work experience and language criteria. If your course shortlist is not eligible for EPOS, do not assume that a general DAAD award can be used instead. Prepare accurate professional and academic evidence for the current application documents.

  1. Read the newest official announcement and select the correct programme pathway.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualifications, experience and any nominated institution.
  3. Check the genuine submission window, documents, financial support and exclusions.
  4. Submit directly using the provider's named application system.
