How the Eiffel scholarship works

The France Excellence Eiffel programme is run by the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs through Campus France. Its most important rule is often missed: students cannot apply directly. A French university or school selects you and submits your file to Campus France; applications sent by students or by foreign universities are ineligible.

The real timeline

Campus France usually publishes the call around the start of October. Universities then set their own internal deadlines, often in November or early December, so they have time to choose candidates before the institutional deadline to Campus France in early January. Results are normally announced in April. If you wait for the Campus France deadline, you will almost always be too late, because the university's internal deadline comes first.

Eligibility points

  • You must be a foreign national applying for a master's or a first-year PhD place at a French institution.
  • Each university can only put forward a limited number of candidates, so you compete inside the university first.
  • Eiffel cannot be combined with another French government scholarship.
  • Age limits and the eligible fields are set in each year's programme rules; check the current version.

Example: A hypothetical engineering graduate admitted to a French master's programme in December may already have missed that university's internal Eiffel deadline. The right move is to contact the international office as soon as you apply for admission and ask for their Eiffel date.

Check the application route

Before preparing an application, confirm citizenship, residence, study level or employment stage, and the correct route for the current cycle. A government scholarship may require a country nomination or university admission; an internship needs a specific job notice and work-location details; a research fellowship may require a host.
