Erasmus+ Youth Exchanges, Traineeships and Mobility

Programme research and source checks, updated 11 November 2026

Programme research only. This page is not an individually verified open vacancy. Follow the provider's latest application notice before treating a date or funding offer as current.

Who the programme is for

Erasmus+ supports several different kinds of mobility. Students can explore traineeships, university exchanges and Erasmus Mundus joint master's degrees, while youth organisations coordinate many short exchange projects. These are not all individual scholarship applications.

Latest official-source information

The European Commission explains that youth exchanges are usually organised by participating groups and that an organisation applies for grant support. The eligible participant age range for youth exchanges is generally 13 to 30, with other requirements applying to group leaders. For a degree or traineeship, the student's institution and specific scheme matter.

Erasmus+ for university students: study periods and traineeships

For students already enrolled at a European university, the most common Erasmus+ routes are a study period at a partner university or a traineeship (internship) at a company or organisation in another participating country. Both are arranged through your own university's Erasmus+ or international office, not by applying to the European Commission.

ItemStudy period or traineeship
Who can take partStudents of any nationality enrolled at a higher education institution that holds the Erasmus Charter (ECHE)
LengthA traineeship lasts 2 to 12 months; a student can spend up to 12 months abroad per study cycle (bachelor's, master's or PhD)
MoneyA monthly grant that depends on the destination country's cost-of-living group, plus extra support for students with fewer opportunities; travel support depends on distance
Recent graduatesCan do a traineeship within 12 months of graduating, but must apply while still enrolled
When to applyYour university sets the deadline; for traineeships it is usually at least two months before the start

Things that commonly go wrong

  • Assuming every European country is covered: some universities point out that Switzerland and the UK are funded through other schemes, not standard Erasmus+ grants.
  • Applying too late: many departments have internal deadlines well before the two-month minimum.
  • Expecting the grant to cover everything: it is a contribution to costs, and if your traineeship already pays as much as the grant, you may only get "zero-grant" status.
  • Confusing Erasmus+ mobility with Erasmus Mundus joint master's degrees, which have their own separate application and scholarship.

The Erasmus Intern traineeship portal, run by the Erasmus Student Network, lists traineeship offers from companies that you can use to find a host.

Example: A hypothetical international master's student at a German university who finds a three-month traineeship in Spain should contact their departmental Erasmus coordinator at least two to three months before the start, sign the learning agreement and then receive the monthly grant for Spain's country group.

How to prepare and avoid eligibility mistakes

Check whether you must apply through a university, a youth organisation or a programme consortium. Review your country of residence, institution participation, destination and the exact support. Distinguish Erasmus+ exchange routes from the separate Erasmus Mundus master's guide so you don't send a single application to the wrong system.

  1. Read the newest official announcement and select the correct programme pathway.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualifications, experience and any nominated institution.
  3. Check the genuine submission window, documents, financial support and exclusions.
  4. Submit directly using the provider's named application system.
