Fulbright Foreign Student Program 2027: Country Applications

Official-provider research reviewed 11 November 2026

Live status: current status, deadline and official source for Fulbright Foreign Student Program – re-checked against the provider.
This is programme research, not an automatically verified open vacancy. Current application eligibility and closing times must be confirmed on the provider's latest official page.

What this programme offers and who it is for

The Fulbright Foreign Student Program supports eligible graduate students, researchers and young professionals from participating countries in study or research in the United States. It is not one centrally administered application for all applicants: binational Fulbright commissions and U.S. embassies manage selection and requirements by country.

Official application status and route

Country eligibility is the first gate. The official U.S. State Department guidance explains that citizenship requirements, available awards and deadlines vary by participating location. You must check the Fulbright commission or embassy responsible for your country, not copy a closing date from another country.

Provider: U.S. Department of State / Fulbright programme. Read official programme instructions ↗

Benefits, placement and the visa rule

The Fulbright Foreign Student Program awards about 4,000 grants a year for master's and doctoral study or research in the United States. What a grant actually pays is set by your country's Fulbright Commission or US Embassy, so two Fulbright grants from different countries can look quite different.

ItemWhat applies
Standard benefitsJ-1 visa sponsorship, funding support, a health benefit plan and enrichment activities
Typical funding (varies by country)Tuition and fees, a monthly living stipend, round-trip airfare, book allowances and health insurance; some countries cap tuition or cover only part of the costs
Where you applyThe Fulbright Commission (about 49 countries) or the US Embassy in your country of nomination, where you must live when applying
University placementEither placement arranged by the Institute of International Education (IIE) or self-placement, where you secure your own US admission; your country decides which
Visa conditionJ-1 visa holders funded by a government programme are generally subject to a two-year home-country physical presence requirement after the grant

The two-year home-country rule

Because Fulbright is government-funded, grantees are generally subject to the US rule (INA 212(e)) that they must spend two years in their home country after the programme before they can get certain US work visas or permanent residence. If you hope to work in the US straight after your degree, understand this rule before accepting the grant.

Placement changes your preparation

If your country uses IIE placement, you give a list of preferred universities and IIE applies on your behalf after you are nominated. With self-placement, you need your own admission, and test scores and statements must meet each university's deadlines. Ask your commission or embassy which model applies before you register for tests.

Example: A hypothetical Pakistani applicant for a US master's degree applies through the United States Educational Foundation in Pakistan, not through any US university. If selected, they would then plan their GRE or other tests around the placement model the Foundation uses, and plan to return home for two years after graduating.

Prepare accurate application evidence

University admission may be managed through different placement arrangements. Some selected applicants use programme-supported placement, while others are responsible for admissions to a particular U.S. institution. Confirm your country's approach before preparing test results, statements and reference documents.

  1. Check the exact provider programme and current application cycle.
  2. Confirm citizenship, degree or experience requirements and any institutional approval.
  3. Record the real funding, costs and application closing time.
  4. Use the official recruitment or admission route, and retain the confirmation.
