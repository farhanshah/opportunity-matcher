GovAI Summer Fellowship 2027: AI Governance Research

What this programme offers and who it is for

GovAI's 2027 UK Summer Fellowship Research Track is a three-month London-based programme for candidates interested in AI governance research and policy analysis. The organiser describes a supervised research project, seminars and opportunities to develop research skills. This differs from a university degree scholarship and from a general software engineering internship.

Official application status and route

The official 2027 Research Track page gives a June to August 2027 programme period and an application deadline of 22 November 2026 at 23:59 UK time. These are provider-published cycle facts checked on 11 November 2026, not a promise that the application remains open at a future visit.

Pay, tracks and timing

GovAI (the Centre for the Governance of AI) runs three-month Seasonal Fellowships in London, twice a year, for people who want to start or move into a career in AI governance and policy. They are paid, full-time and in person.

Research Track or Applied Track

On the Research Track, each fellow designs an independent research project and is paired with a supervisor from GovAI or its network; outputs can be a report, journal article or blog post. On the Applied Track, fellows work on non-research projects pitched by GovAI or partner organisations, which in 2026 included the UK's AI Security Institute. Choose the track that matches the work you want to do after the fellowship.

Who gets selected

There is no fixed degree requirement, and applicants come from policy, law, economics, computer science and other backgrounds. What matters is a clear interest in AI governance, evidence that you can produce careful analysis, and a realistic project idea. Fellows who need a visa should plan to stay in their own country while it is processed.

Example: A hypothetical law graduate working on technology regulation could apply to the Research Track with a focused question, such as how a specific AI law handles open-source models, rather than a broad topic like "AI and society".
