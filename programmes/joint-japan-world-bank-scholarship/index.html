How the Joint Japan/World Bank scholarship works

The Joint Japan/World Bank Graduate Scholarship Program (JJ/WBGSP) is funded by the Government of Japan and administered by the World Bank. It funds mid-career professionals from eligible developing countries to take a development-related master's programme, and it has awarded over 6,800 scholarships across 160 countries.

RequirementWhat the official guidelines say
NationalityA national of a country on the eligible list; no exceptions are made
DegreeA bachelor's degree earned at least three years before the application deadline
ExperienceAt least three years of recent, paid, development-related work after the bachelor's degree
Current jobEmployed full-time, paid, in development-related work when you apply
AdmissionUnconditional admission (except for funding) to a JJ/WBGSP participating master's programme outside your own country
AgeNo minimum or maximum age

What counts as experience, and what does not

The work-experience rule causes most rejections. In one recent annual report, 13 of 15 ineligible applicants lacked the three years of development-related experience. Time spent in graduate study does not count, even if it was development-related, and unpaid internships do not count. Paid work done before your first degree does not count either. The World Bank states that it makes no exceptions to these criteria.

Benefits and limits

The scholarship covers tuition and mandatory fees, a monthly living stipend, round-trip airfare and a travel allowance. It does not pay visa costs or the costs of bringing or supporting family members. People already studying in a participating programme cannot receive it for that programme, and dual-degree programmes with a participating programme are not accepted.

Example: A hypothetical officer who has worked for four years at a national planning ministry since graduating, and is still employed there, could apply after securing unconditional admission to a participating master's programme abroad. Someone with two years of work plus a master's degree would not yet qualify, because the master's years do not count.

A government scholarship may require a country nomination or university admission; an internship needs a specific job notice and work-location details; a research fellowship may require a host.
