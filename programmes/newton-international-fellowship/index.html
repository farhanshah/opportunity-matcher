Newton International Fellowships: eligibility that matters

The Royal Society's Newton International Fellowships support early-career researchers based outside the UK who want to conduct research at a UK host organisation. The Royal Society handles natural-sciences applications; its official instructions direct humanities and social-sciences applicants to the British Academy route. This is a host-supported research fellowship, not a general master's scholarship or a guaranteed work placement.

The Royal Society states that its 2026 scheme round is closed. This page is preparatory research; it does not announce that a later round is available. Always return to the latest scheme announcement before assuming an opening or deadline.

Why the UK sponsor restriction changes your preparation

The sponsor is expected to help develop the proposal and mentor the fellow during the award. Since 2026 the sponsor limit applies to each individual UK academic, not just to the university as a whole. A laboratory with many academics may therefore have capacity, while the supervisor you initially approach might already be supporting another candidate in the same round.

Example: Two researchers proposing different projects to the same UK professor should not assume that both can submit under that sponsor in one round. They need to review the current sponsor rules and find an appropriate available host. A good match requires the host's expertise and agreement, not merely the institution's reputation.

How the award is funded

The official scheme describes a maximum award value of £280,000 over two years, covering eligible salary and institutional on-costs, research expenses and certain relocation or visa costs. This is not a £280,000 personal stipend. Read the scheme's costing rules and agree a realistic budget with the UK host. Do not assume that every budget line or dependent expense will automatically be approved.

How to prepare for a future call

  1. Check your PhD defence and postdoctoral experience timeline against the current rules.
  2. Confirm that your proposed natural-sciences topic fits the Royal Society rather than another funding body's subject remit.
  3. Prepare a specific question, method, feasibility plan, resources and research limitations.
  4. Contact suitable UK sponsors early and ask whether they can support an application under the per-round restriction.
  5. Check the current Flexi-Grant application system and official scheme notes for the required parts and institutional approval steps.
  6. Only after the official call opens, record its precise deadline, references and submission time zone.

The 2026 process changed application parts and reference requirements. Old templates may now be wrong, so always obtain the scheme notes for the exact round you intend to enter.
