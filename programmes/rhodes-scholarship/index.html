How the Rhodes Scholarship works

The Rhodes Scholarship funds postgraduate study at the University of Oxford. Over 100 scholarships are awarded each year through about 25 constituencies (a country or group of countries), plus two Global Rhodes Scholarships for applicants from countries without a constituency. You apply to the Rhodes Trust first; Oxford admission is handled after you are selected.

ItemWhat the official guidance says
FundingOxford University and college fees, an annual living stipend, the Oxford application fee after selection, the student visa fee and economy flights at the start and end of the scholarship
AgeSet by each constituency, generally between 18 and 24, and up to 27 only in exceptional circumstances; the rules are applied strictly
Academic levelUsually a first-class honours degree or a GPA of 3.7/4.0 or equivalent, and meeting the entry requirements of your chosen Oxford course
CitizenshipYou must meet the citizenship and residency rules of the constituency you apply through
TimingApplications roughly June to October, selection October to December, Oxford application December to March, arrival in September; exact dates vary by constituency

Constituency or Global Scholarship?

If your country has a constituency, you must apply through it, using that constituency's Information for Candidates document. The Global Scholarship is only for candidates from countries outside the constituencies, and it requires a nomination from your undergraduate university. Pakistan has its own constituency, which is the route this page's live record tracks.

What selectors look for

Rhodes selection goes beyond grades: committees look for leadership, commitment to others and a clear purpose for studying at Oxford. You will need a CV, a personal statement, an academic statement and several referees, and shortlisted candidates are interviewed. If you are unsuccessful, you can re-apply only once more, and only in the same constituency.

Example: A hypothetical 23-year-old final-year student in Pakistan with strong grades should apply through the Pakistan constituency in the year before they want to start at Oxford. Applying early, in the final year of study, keeps them inside the age limit and leaves one more attempt if the first application fails.

Check the application route

Before preparing an application, confirm citizenship, residence, study level or employment stage, and the correct route for the current cycle. A government scholarship may require a country nomination or university admission; an internship needs a specific job notice and work-location details; a research fellowship may require a host.
