Rotary Peace Fellowship: 2027–28 and Next Cycle

Programme research and source checks, updated 11 November 2026

Live status: current status, deadline and official source for Rotary Peace Fellowship – re-checked against the provider.
Programme research only. This page is not an individually verified open vacancy. Follow the provider's latest application notice before treating a date or funding offer as current.

Who the programme is for

Rotary's peace centres programme supports selected candidates pursuing a master's degree or a professional development certificate in peace and development. These routes are different from ordinary postgraduate research fellowships. Relevant professional experience, evidence of leadership and the course pathway matter in deciding which route fits.

Latest official-source information

The official Rotary page states that applications for the 2027–28 intake have closed and that the next 2028–29 application will be available from February 2027. This is a research page, not an open-application announcement.

Organiser: Rotary International. Read current official instructions ↗

Master's degree or certificate: which fellowship fits

The Rotary Foundation funds up to 50 master's fellowships and up to 50 certificate fellowships a year at its Rotary Peace Centers. They are fully funded, but they are for people already working in peace and development, not for recent graduates without experience.

ItemMaster's degree fellowshipProfessional development certificate
Experience neededAt least three years of relevant full-time workAt least five years of relevant full-time work
Length15 to 24 months, including two to three months of applied field experienceAn intensive programme of about three months with field study (in Bangkok at Chulalongkorn University; also offered in Uganda at Makerere University)
CoversTuition and fees, room and board, round-trip transport, and research and field-study expenses
LanguageEnglish proficiency; a bachelor's degree is required

Who cannot apply

  • Active and honorary Rotary members, and employees of Rotary clubs or districts.
  • Anyone wanting doctoral study: the fellowships do not fund PhDs.
  • Master's fellows cannot study at a Peace Center in their home country.
  • Past Rotary scholars and fellows must wait a set number of years before applying again.

Timing and endorsement

Applications usually open in February and close in May, for study starting the following year. Applications are endorsed by a Rotary district, so contact a local Rotary club early; they can guide you, and many districts interview candidates before endorsing them.

Example: A hypothetical NGO officer with four years of work in refugee protection fits the master's fellowship. A colleague with eight years in mediation who does not want a full degree is a better fit for the certificate programme.

How to prepare and avoid eligibility mistakes

Before applying, compare the master's route with the certificate route. Read Rotary's current rules on work experience, academic background, programme centres, English evidence and any restrictions on Rotary members or family. Record the appropriate centre and complete the official form in the next published cycle.

  1. Read the newest official announcement and select the correct programme pathway.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualifications, experience and any nominated institution.
  3. Check the genuine submission window, documents, financial support and exclusions.
  4. Submit directly using the provider's named application system.
