How the Treasury Summer Internship works

The World Bank Treasury Summer Internship is the only World Bank programme designed specifically for college students, which makes it different from the main World Bank Internship Program for graduate students. It is a paid, full-time, in-person internship in Washington, DC, built around rotations through Treasury teams.

What the application asks for

The Treasury asks for a one-page cover letter and a one-page CV in PDF, with an optional short personal or diversity statement, and it publishes suggested templates. Incomplete applications are not accepted. You must be available full-time for the whole ten weeks without another job or internship at the same time.

How to stand out

Treasury work covers capital markets, investment management, risk and development finance. The strongest applications link a specific Treasury area to concrete evidence: finance or economics coursework, spreadsheet or programming skills, investment clubs or previous internships. Some interns receive offers for junior analyst roles after a successful internship.

Example: A hypothetical international student in the third year of a four-year finance degree should prepare the cover letter and CV in September, apply before the mid-October deadline, and confirm they can be in Washington for the full summer. A final-year student graduating before the internship would normally not fit this programme and should look at graduate-level options instead.
