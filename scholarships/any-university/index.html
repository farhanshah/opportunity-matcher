Scholarships you can use at a university of your choice

These scholarships come from governments and public agencies, not from one university. They are open by nationality and level of study, and you choose (or help choose) where you study. That makes them the first place to look once you know which country you want, and before you rely on a single university's own awards.

Government and agency funding

Source caution: The rules below summarise each scheme's published approach. Eligible countries, fields and deadlines change every cycle, so confirm them on the official page and on each programme's record, which shows its current status.

Scholarships that only work at certain places

Some well-known awards look international but are linked to a specific university, consortium or course list. They are worth applying for, but they will not fund a place at a university of your choice:

  • Rhodes Scholarship: study at the University of Oxford only.
  • Gates Cambridge: the University of Cambridge only.
  • Commonwealth Shared Scholarships: only at the UK universities taking part in that year, and you apply through the university.
  • Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters: you apply to a specific joint programme run by a group of universities, not to the university of your choice.
  • DAAD EPOS: only the postgraduate courses on the EPOS list.

How to use this list

  1. Start with nationality. Most of these schemes are limited to citizens of listed countries. Check the country list before anything else.
  2. Match the level. Chevening funds one-year master's degrees only; the Swiss scheme is for doctoral and postdoctoral research; Stipendium Hungaricum covers bachelor's to PhD.
  3. Check the order of steps. Some schemes want you to apply to universities first (Chevening asks for an offer by its own deadline), while embassy routes such as MEXT and GKS select you first and then place you.
  4. Plan for conditions after the award. Several government scholarships expect you to return home for a period after you finish. Read this before you accept.
  5. Combine with the university's own funding. A university fee discount or bursary can often sit alongside, but some schemes do not allow double funding of the same costs. Ask both sides.

Example: A hypothetical applicant from Pakistan with three years of work experience wants a one-year MSc at a UK university that offers no large award of its own. Pakistan is a Commonwealth country and is eligible for Chevening, so the realistic options are Chevening (with that university as one of the three choices) and the Commonwealth Master's Scholarship, if the course fits the Commission's priorities. Both are applied for outside the university, and both can be used there.
