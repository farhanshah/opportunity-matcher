Nationality is not the same as residence or study location

Scholarship rules can refer to citizenship, ordinary residence, immigration status, place of previous study, or a combination of those facts. An award for study in a country is not automatically limited to citizens of that country, and the phrase “international applicants” does not prove that every passport is eligible.

Illustrative example: A Pakistani citizen studying at a UK university should not automatically apply through a UK-national applicant route. The relevant award may classify candidates by passport, habitual residence or current institution. Only the provider's exact eligibility rules can settle the route.

Prepare the details once, then assess every award separately

Keep a private record of your citizenship, residence periods, institution, qualification dates, intended course, subject and any language evidence. Compare these with the named programme's current conditions. You do not need to publish sensitive documents on an intermediary website to check ordinary eligibility rules.

  1. Locate the actual sponsor and current round's eligible-country wording.
  2. Check whether the award is for international students, residents or a narrower specified group.
  3. Separate tuition eligibility from the award's funding and academic requirements.
  4. Identify the proper country nomination or university route where required.
  5. Use the official portal and follow the sponsor's accessible application process.
