The three main ways international students fund study in Germany

Germany has no single national scholarship application. Funding comes from the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD), from universities through the Deutschlandstipendium, and from foundations that support gifted students. The right route depends on your degree level, nationality and whether you are already admitted.

DAAD: start with the scholarship database

The DAAD scholarship database lists DAAD programmes and selected offers from other funders, filtered by your country of origin and subject. Deadlines differ by country and programme, so the same scholarship can close at different times for applicants from different countries. EPOS (Development-Related Postgraduate Courses), for example, is for graduates from eligible developing countries with at least two years of professional experience, and it funds only the postgraduate courses on the EPOS list, for 12 to 42 months.

Deutschlandstipendium: the most overlooked route

International students are eligible on the same terms as German students if they are enrolled, or applying to enrol, at a participating university. Doctoral and exchange students are excluded, and you usually cannot combine it with another merit-based scholarship worth 30 euros or more per month. Each university runs its own call, often once a year.

Tuition is not always zero

Most public universities charge no tuition for most programmes, but students pay a semester contribution, and some states (such as Baden-Württemberg) charge tuition to most non-EU students. Check the fee rules of the exact university and state before assuming a scholarship only needs to cover living costs.

Example: A hypothetical public-policy graduate with three years of work experience in an eligible country could check whether their target course appears on the EPOS list. If it does not, EPOS cannot fund it, and the realistic options become a regular DAAD graduate scholarship, the university's Deutschlandstipendium after admission, or a foundation.

Official sources: DAAD scholarship database and Deutschlandstipendium.

How to check eligibility

For Germany, filter the DAAD database by your own country first, then check your target university's Deutschlandstipendium call and fee rules.
