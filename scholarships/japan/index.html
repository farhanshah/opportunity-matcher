MEXT, JASSO and university funding compared

Japan's largest scholarship for international students is the Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship. Students who come to Japan without MEXT can still apply for JASSO's Honors Scholarship and for private foundation awards through their university. These are different programmes with different timing, so it helps to know which one you are actually applying for.

How the embassy route works

The Japanese embassy or consulate in your country runs the first screening: document review, written tests and an interview, depending on the country. Applications go to the embassy, never directly to MEXT. Candidates who pass the first screening then ask Japanese universities for a letter of provisional acceptance. For the 2027 research-student intake, embassies published guidelines in spring 2026, with deadlines that varied by country.

Research student status is not a degree

Many MEXT scholars start as non-degree research students and must pass the university's entrance examination to move into a master's or doctoral programme. If you fail it, you can remain a research student only within the scholarship period. Read the "academic pathway" section of the guideline before choosing between starting as a research student and applying directly to a degree.

Example: A hypothetical applicant over 35 cannot use the MEXT research-student route. They could instead apply to a Japanese graduate school as a privately financed student and then seek a JASSO Honors Scholarship or a foundation award after enrolment.

Official sources: Study in Japan (official portal) and the Japan Student Services Organization (JASSO).

How to check eligibility

MEXT embassy and university nomination routes use different processes. Confirm your country's official announcement and internal dates. For Japan, check the MEXT guideline published by the Japanese embassy in your own country, because the screening schedule and tests are set locally.

  1. Check the official source and exact programme identity.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualification and location restrictions.
  3. Verify admissions, nominations and document requirements.
  4. Record the final provider deadline and funding terms.
