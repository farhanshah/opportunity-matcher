Research and PhD scholarships

Separate doctoral studentships from postdoctoral research awards

A PhD studentship usually supports a student completing a doctoral degree. A postdoctoral fellowship is commonly designed for applicants who already hold, or are about to complete, a doctorate. A grant may be awarded to a research institution rather than to the individual. These labels can overlap, but the exact applicant stage on the official notice is decisive.

Funding typeTypical application evidenceEligibility question
Advertised PhD projectAcademic record, subject fit and research or laboratory skillsDoes the studentship fund international tuition rates and your nationality?
Open-topic doctoral fundingResearch question, methodology, supervisor agreement and degree admissionDo you secure admission or a host before requesting the award?
Postdoctoral fellowshipCompleted PhD timing, outputs, host sponsor and detailed work planDoes it permit your career stage and mobility or residence history?

Illustrative example: A master's graduate interested in food safety finds a “research fellowship” that requires a PhD awarded before the application deadline. The topic may fit their interests but the qualification rule does not. They should compare doctoral studentships rather than treating the programme title as sufficient evidence of eligibility.

What a research proposal needs to explain

Start with one question that can be answered within the award's time and resources. Name the data or equipment needed, a realistic sampling approach, how observations will be analysed and the limitations. “I will solve food security” is a goal, not a research design; a defined study of shelf-life evidence for a particular food under specified conditions can be evaluated by a supervisor.

Before contacting a host, read the current funding notice and check whether you must obtain institutional approval. A prospective supervisor's willingness to discuss your idea is not a scholarship offer. Some awards pay a stipend; others cover tuition or fund the host's research costs. Separate those components.

  1. Confirm whether you are applying for a doctoral degree, postdoctoral position or research grant.
  2. Check the award's degree dates, nationality, study location and required host.
  3. Match your methods and academic evidence to the named project or theme.
  4. Record university admission, sponsor sign-off and the funding deadline separately.
  5. Apply only through the official university or funder's route.
