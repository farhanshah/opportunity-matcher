How the Global Korea Scholarship is organised

Most fully funded study in South Korea for international students runs through the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), administered by the National Institute for International Education (NIIED) under the Korean Ministry of Education. GKS is split into two separate competitions: GKS-U for undergraduate degrees and GKS-G for master's, doctoral and short research programmes. Each competition then has two application tracks, and you can only use one of them in a given year.

TrackWhere you applyBest fitWatch out for
Embassy trackThe Korean embassy (or a designated body) in your country of citizenshipApplicants whose country has an embassy quota and who want to choose universities laterQuotas differ by country; some embassies run their own first-round interview or test
University trackDirectly to one designated Korean universityApplicants who already know the department and supervisor they wantEach university sets its own internal deadline and document format; you cannot also apply via the embassy

Benefits and structure

Graduate awards cover round-trip economy airfare, full tuition, a monthly living allowance, a one-time settlement allowance and, normally, one year of Korean language training before the degree. A doctoral award is typically four years: one year of language study plus three years of degree study. Exact allowance amounts, insurance and completion grants are set in each year's official guideline, so copy them from the current document rather than from older blog posts.

Eligibility points that cause most rejections

  • Citizenship: both the applicant and their parents must hold non-Korean citizenship.
  • Age: GKS-G applicants must be under 40; GKS-U applicants must be under 25 on the cut-off date stated in the guideline.
  • Degree level: a bachelor's degree is required for master's study and a master's degree for doctoral study, completed by the date the guideline specifies.
  • University type: embassy-track candidates choose from the universities and types listed for that track only.

Typical timing

GKS-G guidelines are usually published around February, with university-track windows at Korean universities running roughly from February to the end of March. GKS-U guidelines are usually published around September for study starting the following year. Embassy deadlines can be earlier than university deadlines, so record the deadline of the exact office you apply through.

Example: A hypothetical engineering graduate wants a master's degree in Korea. If their country has an embassy quota, they could apply through the embassy and list preferred universities. If they have already contacted a professor at one university, the university track may suit them better, but they then give up the embassy route for that year.

Official sources: Study in Korea (NIIED) GKS pages and the Korean embassy website in your country.

How to check eligibility

Global Korea Scholarship distinguishes undergraduate and graduate routes. Check the current embassy or university route and applicant-country requirements. For GKS, confirm which competition (GKS-U or GKS-G) and which track (embassy or university) you are using before preparing documents, because each track has its own deadline and form.

  1. Check the official source and exact programme identity.
  2. Confirm citizenship, qualification and location restrictions.
  3. Verify admissions, nominations and document requirements.
  4. Record the final provider deadline and funding terms.
