How to find the right programme

Study funding in the United States is spread across academic departments, external foundations, graduate assistantships and country-specific government awards. There is no single national form covering all universities. Check whether the award is linked to an admission application or separate sponsor competition.

For graduate-level opportunities, look at faculty funding, assistantship eligibility and the applicant's existing qualification. Some well-known awards are open only to certain citizenships or study stages. A full tuition waiver can still leave required insurance or living costs.

For undergraduate study, compare institutional aid and admission requirements before selecting a university. A reference to 'fully funded' is not a substitute for the official costs table and conditions for international students.

Where funding for international students in the US actually comes from

International students cannot use US federal student aid, so funding comes from three places: the universities themselves, academic departments that hire graduate students, and a small number of external programmes such as Fulbright. Which one matters depends mostly on whether you want an undergraduate or a graduate degree.

Undergraduate: need-blind versus need-aware

Most US universities are need-aware for international applicants, which means asking for financial aid can affect the admission decision. A small number are need-blind and also meet full demonstrated need for international students; these are among the most competitive universities in the world. Read each university's international aid page rather than relying on lists, because policies change from year to year.

Graduate: funding usually comes with admission

Most funded PhD students in the US are supported by a package from their department: a tuition waiver plus a stipend for teaching or research work. Master's degrees are funded much less often. Before you accept, check whether the offer is guaranteed for several years or renewed each year. Our assistantship versus fellowship guide explains the difference.

Fulbright is run from your home country

Fulbright for non-US students is not applied for in the US. Binational Fulbright Commissions run it in about 49 countries and US Embassies elsewhere, so eligibility, timing and selection differ by country. Around 4,000 foreign students receive Fulbright grants each year.

Example: A hypothetical chemistry graduate who wants a US PhD should apply directly to departments and ask about assistantship funding, and can also apply for Fulbright in their home country. A Fulbright award and a departmental offer can sometimes be combined, but the student needs to check the rules of both.

Official sources: Foreign Fulbright and the international financial aid page of each university.

What to check before applying

  1. Identify the latest official programme or individual vacancy announcement.
  2. Check applicant citizenship, residence, course or career stage and nomination requirements.
  3. Record exact deadlines, documents, conditions and funding from the current source.
